The Key Setting

The Canon R6 Mark II has a specific low light advantage that most operators never activate — the AF assist beam. In genuinely dark environments where contrast detection struggles to find an edge to lock onto the R6 Mark II can emit a brief AF assist pulse that gives the autofocus system enough contrast to acquire the subject. Find it under the AF menu, AF assist beam firing, and set it to Enable. Use it selectively — in social event environments the brief pulse is unobtrusive but in quiet ceremonial moments switch it off to remain invisible. Additionally the R6 Mark II's subject detection maintains face and eye lock in significantly lower light than competing systems because of its dual pixel AF II architecture which reads phase detection information across the entire sensor surface rather than a dedicated AF zone. In practice this means the R6 Mark II finds and holds faces in candlelit and near-dark environments where other cameras lose lock and hunt. Trust the system. Keep Face and Eye AF active even when the environment feels too dark for it to work — it will surprise you.
