The Key Setting

Low light real estate is the scenario where the Fujifilm X-T5's 40 megapixel sensor produces its most extraordinary results — and this is the complete reversal of the X-T5's low light sports limitations. On a tripod at base ISO 160 with maximum depth of field aperture the high resolution sensor that struggles in high ISO handheld scenarios becomes the most detailed architectural imaging tool in this database. At f8-f11 on a stable tripod the X-T5 resolves wall texture, floor grain, window frame detail, and furniture surface quality at a level that no other camera in this database matches at equivalent price points. The 40 megapixel files at base ISO 160 in long exposure real estate work produce images that compete with medium format digital cameras costing ten times more. To maximize this advantage shoot in the X-T5's lowest compression RAW format — go to shooting menu, image quality, select RAF uncompressed. The uncompressed RAW files at base ISO 160 retain every photon the sensor captured during the long exposure and produce architectural detail in post processing that compressed RAW formats cannot fully recover. Additionally use the X-T5's interval timer for bracketing sequences in low light real estate — go to shooting menu, interval timer shooting, set interval to three seconds to allow mirror and shutter vibration to fully dissipate between frames, and set shot count to three for a standard three frame bracket. The three second interval on the X-T5 is longer than competing cameras require because the X-T5's mechanical shutter produces more vibration during the exposure sequence than Sony and Canon equivalents — the additional interval time ensures each bracketed frame is captured at maximum stability.
