Sony A7IV Sports Golden Hour Settings

The Key Setting

Golden hour sports—late‑afternoon soccer, sunset track meets, golden‑hour tennis—offer beautiful directional light but demand that the camera track fast, erratic movement through rapidly changing exposure. The Sony A7IV’s 10 fps mechanical burst is fast enough for most sports, but its real strength is the AF system inherited from the A1/A7R V lineage. The tracking is sticky and reliable when properly configured. For golden hour action, set AF Track Sens to 2 (Responsive) — this tells the camera to quickly react when a subject changes speed or direction, which is essential for sports where athletes accelerate, cut, or jump unpredictably. Use AF Area set to Tracking: Expand Spot — place the initial focus point on your subject, and the camera will track them across the frame. Enable Subject Detection set to Human.
The golden hour light brings one significant challenge: when an athlete moves between bright sunlight and long shadows, the camera’s metering can lag behind the sudden exposure shift. The A7IV’s Auto ISO in Shutter Priority mode handles this better than most competitors, but you can help it by setting the Metering Mode to Highlight — this biases the exposure toward protecting the bright golden highlights on the subject’s skin and kit, preventing blown‑out jerseys or faces. The shadows will naturally deepen, which adds drama to golden hour sports images. Enable Zebra Display set to 100+ to warn you before highlights clip.
The A7IV is not a dedicated sports body. Its 10 fps burst is adequate but not class‑leading, and the buffer fills relatively quickly when shooting uncompressed RAW. For golden hour sports, shoot Compressed RAW or JPEG (Fine) to extend the buffer. The A7IV rewards anticipation and timing over spray‑and‑pray. Use the golden light to your advantage: position yourself with the sun behind you or at a 45‑degree angle, so the warm light illuminates the athletes’ faces and creates rim light on their movement.
