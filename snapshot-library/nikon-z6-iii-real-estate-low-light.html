The Key Setting

The Nikon Z6 III's focus shift shooting feature — introduced in the standard Real Estate Indoor Artificial entry — becomes the most powerful tool in the entire low light real estate workflow when combined with long exposure technique. In standard indoor real estate shooting focus stacking at f8 produces edge to edge sharpness superior to single exposures at f11 or f16. In low light real estate this advantage compounds significantly because the exposure duration required at f16 to achieve equivalent depth of field through aperture alone is dramatically longer than at f8 — and longer exposures in dim listings increase the risk of subject movement from HVAC vibration, traffic, and building settling producing blur across the entire frame. Shooting at f8 with focus stacking keeps exposure times shorter while producing superior depth of field compared to stopping down. Go to photo shooting menu, focus shift shooting, set number of shots to fifteen for low light real estate work, focus step width to three, and interval to three seconds — the longer interval than daylight shooting accounts for the longer individual exposure times ensuring each frame in the stack is fully captured before the next begins. Additionally the Z6 III's partially stacked sensor shadow recovery in low light real estate produces interior shadow detail in corners and under furniture that competing cameras cannot recover at equivalent ISO values — combine the focus stack with a separate long exposure bracket for the window and blend all elements in Lightroom for a finished image with edge to edge sharpness, retained window detail, and clean shadow recovery that represents the absolute ceiling of what mirrorless real estate photography can produce.
