The Key Setting

The Sony A6700’s AI‑powered autofocus system—inherited from the flagship A7R V—is the camera’s greatest strength in golden hour portraiture. The Human Pose Estimation feature means the camera predicts where the subject’s head and eyes will be, even when the face is partially obscured by warm flare or turned slightly away. In backlit golden hour scenarios, set Subject Detection to Human, Face/Eye Priority in AF to On, and let the AI handle the tracking. The A6700 will hold focus on the subject’s eye through warm, flaring light that can confuse lesser AF systems.
The APS‑C sensor means you need wider apertures to achieve the same depth‑of‑field look as full‑frame. A 56mm f/1.4 lens on the A6700 (approximately 84mm full‑frame equivalent) provides beautiful subject isolation at golden hour. The A6700’s compact size is a genuine advantage during a portrait session—it feels less imposing, and subjects relax more quickly, which is particularly important during the fleeting golden hour window when you need genuine expressions, not stiff poses.
Sony’s Creative Look profiles are worth exploring for golden hour. The FL (Film Look) Creative Look adds a cinematic, slightly muted warmth that prevents skin tones from appearing artificially orange while retaining the golden light’s emotional quality. The VV (Vivid) Creative Look can be used sparingly for high‑energy golden hour portraits where you want the warm colours to pop, but reduce Saturation by 1 to avoid oversaturation.
For backlit portraits, use Spot Metering linked to the focus point. The A6700 will meter for the subject’s face, allowing the golden background to brighten naturally into a glowing wash. Enable Zebra Display set to 100+ to monitor highlight clipping on the rim light; the A6700’s sensor has decent dynamic range for an APS‑C body, but you want to protect the brightest parts of the subject’s skin from clipping.
