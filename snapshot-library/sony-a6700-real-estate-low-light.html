The Key Setting

The Sony A6700 in low light real estate combines two challenges simultaneously — the APS-C crop factor wide angle limitation and the need for long exposure technique in dim listings. Both are solvable with the correct preparation before arriving at the property. The crop factor solution is identical to the standard Real Estate entry — the Sigma 10-18mm f2.8 DC DN or Sony E 10-20mm f4 G PZ for full frame equivalent 15-27mm coverage. In low light real estate this lens choice becomes even more critical because dim listings often cannot be fully illuminated by the available light sources and the photographer cannot move furniture or reposition the camera significantly without disrupting the scene. Arriving with the correct wide angle lens is the only solution — there is no post processing fix for a room that could not fit in the frame. Once the lens situation is correct the A6700's long exposure capability at base ISO 100 produces real estate files of a quality that surprises photographers switching from crop sensor systems — at ISO 100 on a stable tripod the A6700's sensor produces clean architectural detail that competes with full frame cameras at equivalent base ISO settings because read noise at base ISO is minimized on both sensor sizes simultaneously. The light painting technique covered in the A7IV low light real estate entry applies equally to the A6700 — bulb mode, remote shutter release, off-camera LED panel walked through the space during the exposure. Go to shooting mode, set shutter speed to BULB, connect a remote release, open the shutter, and walk through the room painting light into shadow areas that ambient sources cannot reach. The A6700 captures the painted light identically to any full frame camera in bulb mode — sensor size does not affect long exposure light painting capability. Additionally enable the A6700's Sweep Panorama mode for rooms where even 10mm cannot capture the full space in low light — go to shooting mode dial, Scene, Sweep Panorama, and pan slowly across the room. The A6700 stitches the panorama in camera and delivers a single wide image even in dim conditions where standard wide angle coverage falls short.
