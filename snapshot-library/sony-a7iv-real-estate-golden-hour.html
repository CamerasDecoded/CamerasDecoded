The Key Setting

Golden hour real estate photography—the hour before sunset—transforms ordinary properties into emotionally compelling listings. The A7IV’s 33‑megapixel sensor captures the warm, directional light with enough resolution to reveal texture in wood, stone, and fabric that lesser cameras blur. For interior golden hour shots, position the camera to capture the golden light streaming through windows as it paints warm pools across floors and walls. Use Aperture Priority at f/8–f/11 on a tripod, with ISO locked at 100 for maximum dynamic range. The slow shutter speeds required (often 1/30s or slower) demand a tripod—the A7IV’s IBIS is excellent, but real estate photography is about absolute sharpness, and no stabilisation system can match a locked‑down tripod for critical architectural detail.
Enable the Electronic Level in the viewfinder (assign to a custom button) to keep verticals perfectly straight. Tilted walls or doorframes are the single most common amateur mistake in real estate photography, and the A7IV’s level helps you avoid them. For high‑dynamic‑range interiors—rooms with bright golden windows and darker corners—use the A7IV’s DRO/Auto HDR setting. DRO Lv5 will automatically lift shadows while preserving the golden highlights, giving a balanced JPEG straight out of camera. For maximum quality, bracket three exposures (±1.5 EV) and blend later in Lightroom or Photoshop. The A7IV’s 33‑megapixel sensor provides enough resolution to crop or correct perspective without losing listing‑quality detail.
For exterior twilight shots—the “golden hour exterior” that real estate agents love—shoot just as the sun dips below the horizon. The warm ambient light balances with any interior lights now visible through the windows. Use f/8, ISO 100, and whatever shutter speed the light demands (often 1–5 seconds on a tripod). Enable Long Exposure NR to reduce noise in the shadow areas. The result is a listing photo that feels warm, inviting, and professionally produced.
