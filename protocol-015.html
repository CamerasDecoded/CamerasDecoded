Using AI for Culling: What Actually Works

A practical guide to AI culling tools — what they're good at, and where they still fail.

015
OPERATOR PROTOCOL

“4,200 frames from a wedding. I started culling at 9pm with coffee and finished at 2am with regret. Somewhere around frame 2,800 my standards collapsed and I started keeping everything — which meant the client got 900 photos including, I discovered later, eleven of the back of the DJ's head.”

The Myth

You've been told AI can cull your wedding — upload, wait, deliver. It can't. AI doesn't know that the slightly soft photo of the grandmother laughing is the most important frame of the day, because importance isn't in the pixels.

The Truth

AI culling is a brilliant bouncer and a terrible editor. It reliably kills the technical failures — blinks, motion blur, misfires, near-duplicates — which is roughly 60% of a wedding's dead weight. What it cannot do is choose: the emotional selects, the storytelling sequence, the frame that's imperfect and perfect. Use it for rejection, never for selection.

The Operator's Protocol — Cull With AI in 3 Steps
01
Run the reject pass. Let the AI flag blinks, blur, and duplicates. Delete with confidence — this is janitorial work, and machines are excellent janitors.
02
You make the selects. From what survives, choose by feeling: the laugh, the glance, the moment. No algorithm has ever cried at a wedding photo. You have.
03
Never auto-deliver. The AI's gallery is a draft, not a delivery. Your eye signs off on every frame the client sees — that's what they're paying for.
Pro tip: Tune the AI's strictness down, not up. A conservative reject pass that keeps too much beats an aggressive one that deletes the grandmother laughing — you can delete later, but you can't undelete.
The Payoff

Culling drops from five hours to ninety minutes, and the selects get better — because your fresh eyes spend their energy on choosing, not on deleting blinks. You didn't need to suffer through 4,200 frames by hand. You needed a bouncer and an editor, and you're the editor.
