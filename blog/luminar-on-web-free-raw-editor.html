RAW Editing Just Moved Into Your Browser, Free to Start

October 17, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: Luminar on Web is a new browser-based photo editor from Skylum that handles RAW files with no install and no subscription. The basic version is free forever, and the full toolset is a one-time $69 purchase. It ships with RAW editing, object removal, photo restoration, AI tools, presets and LUTs, and cross-device sync, aimed squarely at beginners who want real editing power without a steep learning curve.

What is Luminar on Web?

Luminar on Web is a photo editor that runs entirely in a browser tab. According to Skylum, the company behind Luminar, it works on any device with a browser: no install, no hardware requirements, no eating up your device's storage. You open the website, upload a photo, and start editing, including high-resolution images and RAW files.

The headline feature is that RAW support. Real RAW editing is still rare among web-based editors, and it is the one thing that separates a toy filter app from a tool you can actually learn on.

What can it actually do?

The toolset is deeper than you would expect from a browser tab. Beyond RAW editing, there is GenErase for removing unwanted objects, Photo Restoration for bringing older or damaged photos back, and Light Depth, which lets you shape lighting to add depth and dimension, Skylum says it is the first browser-based editor with that technology.

It also carries Luminar's AI tools, Sky, Structure, Body, and Bokeh, the same ones in the downloadable software, plus presets and LUTs side by side. Skylum claims it is the first online editor to offer presets and LUTs together. Edits sync across devices through a shared Prime asset library, so you can start on a Chromebook and finish on a phone.

What does it cost?

The basic version is free, and you can keep using it like that forever if the basic functions cover your needs. Full access to Luminar on Web is a $69 one-time purchase, no subscription. Skylum says it is the first and only photo editor with a lifetime license across all its options: desktop, mobile, and web.

Why it matters for you

Learning to edit RAW files is where a photographer learns to see light. It is also where most beginners stall, because the traditional path demands an install, a subscription, and a machine with enough storage. A free browser tab with real RAW editing removes all three excuses at once.

The low-friction version of this advice is simple: shoot a few RAW frames this week and try editing one of them in a browser. You will learn more about exposure in twenty minutes of pushing shadows and highlights than in a week of reading about the exposure triangle. The best editing teacher is a slider you can actually move, and now it is free.
