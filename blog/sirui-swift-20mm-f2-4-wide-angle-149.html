A $149 Wide-Angle Prime That Makes the Expensive Ones Hard to Justify

November 16, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: Digital Camera World gives the Sirui Swift 20mm f/2.4 a 4.5 out of 5: a $149 full-frame ultra-wide that is compact at 207 grams, sharp stopped down, and fast to focus, with an aperture ring, a function button, and weather sealing. For Sony E, L-mount, and Fujifilm X shooters, it is the cheapest honest way into ultra-wide.

What do you actually get for $149?

A 20mm prime with a 91.5-degree angle of view, an f/2.4 to f/16 aperture, and a body that measures 70 by 62 millimeters and weighs 207 grams. It comes in Sony E, L-mount, and Fujifilm X, and it is full-frame compatible. The extras are the surprise: a dedicated aperture ring with 1/3-stop clicks and an Auto position, a customizable function button, an AF/MF switch, extensive weather sealing including a gasket on the metal mount, a USB-C port for firmware updates, and a 0.2-meter minimum focus distance. A petal hood, front cap, drawstring pouch, and 3-year warranty come in the box. That list reads like a lens twice the price, and the ring and button matter most for learning: they put exposure and focus control under your fingers, not in a menu.

How does it perform where it counts?

The reviewer's lab tests and 27 sample images, shot in Wells, England on a 33-megapixel Sony A7 V, tell a consistent story. At medium-to-narrow apertures like f/5.6, sharpness is excellent in the center and holds up well to the edges and corners. Interiors show good distortion and color-fringing control, and wide-open shots show minimal chromatic aberration. The lab measured sharpness as very good in the center and mid-frame, softer only in the extreme corners, with minor barrel distortion. Autofocus is swift and accurate for stills, smooth and near-silent for video. Best uses are landscapes, cityscapes, architectural interiors, vlogging at arm's length, and astrophotography. On Fujifilm X it is not ultra-wide at all, giving a 35mm-equivalent field of view, so there it suits street and environmental portraiture instead.

What are you giving up?

Less than you would expect, but the gaps are real. There is no click and de-click switch for the aperture ring, so video shooters cannot make stepless aperture transitions. There is no lock for the Auto position on the ring. And there is no Nikon Z version yet. The honest comparison is the Sigma 20mm f/2 DG DN Contemporary at $769: faster, all-metal, nearly twice the weight, and no Fujifilm X version. The Viltrox AF 20mm f/2.8 Air at $168 is marginally smaller and lighter, but has no aperture ring, function button, or AF/MF switch. The Sirui sits between them on price and ahead of both on handling, which is an unusual sentence to write about a $149 lens.

Why it matters for you

The beginner's wide angle used to be a slow kit zoom or a prime that cost more than the camera it sat on. A $149 prime with an aperture ring changes the arithmetic: you can learn to see wide without saving for months first. And the money you do not spend is a trip somewhere worth pointing it at.
