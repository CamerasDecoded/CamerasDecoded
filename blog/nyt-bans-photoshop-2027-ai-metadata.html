The New York Times Just Banned the New Photoshop. The Reason Matters More Than the Ban

November 25, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: The New York Times has told freelance photographers to avoid Photoshop 2027 because certain functions use generative AI in ways that may not be apparent, and the files can carry metadata identifying them as AI-generated. For learners, editing choices now leave a visible record, and platforms rarely distinguish a small cleanup from a generated image.

What did the Times actually ban?

The Times did not ban AI in the abstract. It banned a specific version of a specific tool. In an email to freelance photographers, confirmed by The Verge and reported by DPReview and PetaPixel, the paper said certain functions in Photoshop 2027 utilize generative AI in ways that may not be apparent to users, and contributors must avoid the new version on assignment. Photoshop 2027 is version 28.0.0. Photographers who already updated can roll back to version 27.0 through Creative Cloud, but no earlier.

That precision matters. The paper has long required that published photos be accurate depictions of real events: no people or objects added, rearranged, blurred, distorted, or removed, and any use of AI must be disclosed. The new policy is about inadvertent use. A photographer who clicks a familiar tool, does not realize it now runs generative AI, and files the picture anyway has created a problem the desk cannot easily undo.

Why does the metadata matter more than the pixels?

Because the label travels farther than the edit. When a tool uses generative AI, that fact can be written into the file metadata, and social platforms can surface an AI warning on the published image. An AI flag on a news photo from an outlet that has said it will not use AI-generated images in reporting would stir controversy even if the underlying edit was minor. Platforms typically do not differentiate between a small cleanup and wholesale generation.

Adobe has offered generative features for years, including Generative Fill and the Remove tool, and it is generally upfront about which features use them. The concern now is creep. Generative AI has moved into more places, including Adobe Camera Raw, where a Remove panel checkbox controls generative AI for one tool without making clear whether it governs the tools below it. The policy says at this time, which suggests guidance may come later. Until then, avoidance is simpler than auditing every click.

Why it matters for you

You do not need to shoot for the Times to feel this. If you post work publicly, enter contests, or deliver to clients, your files now carry a record of how they were made, and that record can be read by people who were not in the room. Learn which of your tools are generative and which are not, and decide on purpose. Removing a dust spot and generating a new sky are different acts, even if the software puts them one click apart. The photographers who keep trust will be the ones who can say, plainly, what they did to a picture and why. That discipline starts before the edit, with a frame that needs less fixing in the first place.
