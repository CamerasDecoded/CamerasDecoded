DJI Now Sells More Cameras Than Every Japanese Maker Combined

October 1, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: DJI shipped 10.4 million handheld cameras in 2025 — more than Canon, Nikon, Sony, and Fujifilm sold combined. The handheld market hit 16.65 million units, up 83 percent in a year. Both markets are growing, but the split matters for learning: a gimbal camera teaches movement, not the exposure triangle.

How big is the handheld camera market now?

New research from IDC puts a number on something you have probably felt: the “handheld smart camera” market — action cameras, 360 cameras, and gimbal cameras like the DJI Osmo Pocket and Insta360 Luna — reached 16.65 million units in 2025, up 83 percent in a single year. DJI alone shipped 10.4 million of them, crossing the ten-million mark for the first time.

For comparison, the entire traditional digital camera market — every interchangeable-lens body and compact from the Japanese makers, tracked by CIPA — sold 9.44 million units in 2025. That was itself a strong year: the fourth straight year of growth and the highest total since 2019. Both markets are growing. One is just growing much, much faster.

Does this mean mirrorless is dying?

This is not one camera killing another. A Pocket and a mirrorless body do different jobs, and plenty of shooters own both. But the split matters for how you learn.

Why it matters for you

Match the tool to the skill you want. A gimbal camera will teach you movement, framing on the fly, and storytelling rhythm. It will not teach you the exposure triangle — there is no aperture ring to turn, no shutter to feel. If your goal is to learn photography as a craft, the interchangeable-lens camera is still the classroom. Buy the tool that teaches what you came to learn.
