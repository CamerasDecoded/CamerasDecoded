Your Memory Card Can Lie to You and Still Pass Every Check

November 26, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: Counterfeit camera cards fake their capacity and speed in firmware, so they appear to work until files vanish or video modes refuse to record. Test every new card by filling and reading it back and by measuring its sustained speed, and buy from a brand-name retailer where the seller cannot change between clicks.

Why does a fake card look like a real one?

The most dangerous gear failure is the one that looks like success. In a Fstoppers test published November 25, a photographer bought six SanDisk memory cards from Amazon and eBay, and every one of them was fake. One set was sold as refurbished high-speed cards with the second row of pins you would expect on a UHS-II card. They looked real. They were not.

You cannot settle it by looking. When the author cut a card open, about 75 percent of the interior was empty space with the working part at the top, exactly what a genuine card looks like inside. The outside is no better, because SanDisk changes card and box designs regularly, so a real card bought direct can carry a logo that looks wrong. George Pavel of SalvageData, quoted in the piece, traces fakes to low-grade chips that failed quality control, bought in bulk for less than a dollar each and dressed in convincing packaging. The most counterfeited brands he sees are Lexar and Samsung, often bought on eBay.

How do you test a card before you trust it?

Fake cards counterfeit two numbers: capacity and speed. Your camera does not measure storage. It reads a number stored in the card firmware, so a card that claims 512GB or 1TB can hold far less, accept files without error, and simply not have them when you go to open them. Pavel described a wedding photographer whose card showed JPEG thumbnails while she believed she was shooting raw. The raw files were never there.

Two free checks cover both lies. On a Mac, F3XSwift fills the entire card with data and reads every bit back to confirm it survived. Checking a 512GB card took about an hour and a half, which is the point: do it the week the card arrives, not the night before a shoot. For speed, Blackmagic Disk Speed Test measures sustained read and write rates. Video exposes a fake fastest. A V90 card is guaranteed never to drop below 90 MB/s, and certain Sony formats will only record to V90 media. The genuine card passed. The look-alike refurbished cards were refused on the spot. Buy accordingly: marketplace listings can swap sellers between refreshes, with roughly 20 sellers attached to a single listing in one example. A brand-name retailer removes that variable.

Why it matters for you

Treat the card as the negative, not as an accessory. The author of the test shot weddings professionally for years on a card that later failed and turned out to be fake. There was no wedding on it when it failed, which is luck, not a system. If you are learning the craft, build the test into the purchase: fill-test new cards, speed-test anything you will use for video, and keep a second card from a different purchase in the bag. The Arena judges the photo you made. A client pays for the photos you can still open. Both depend on the cheapest part of your kit telling the truth.
