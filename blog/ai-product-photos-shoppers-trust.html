Real Product Photos Still Win the Sale

November 3, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: Most online shoppers say AI-generated product images make them less confident about buying, and one in six would walk away from the purchase entirely. A product photograph is not decoration to a buyer. It is the evidence they use to judge color, fit, texture, and scale. Real photography is what turns a listing into a sale.

What did shoppers actually say?

E-commerce research group Baymard surveyed 2,273 online shoppers in the United States in June 2026 and asked how they would react if they knew a product image was AI-generated. Sixty percent said it would reduce their confidence in the purchase: 25 percent slightly, 18 percent significantly, and 17 percent said they would not feel confident enough to buy at all. Only 18 percent said it would make no difference.

The timing matters. Retailers are turning to generative AI for product imagery, including lifestyle shots and virtual models, largely to cut photography costs. The savings are real, and so is the backlash: last month a food delivery app apologized for inaccurate AI-generated menu photos, and fashion photo studios are closing as brands replace shoots with generated images.

Why does a generated photo cost a sale?

Baymard co-founder Christian Holst puts it plainly: shoppers do not treat product photos as decoration. They use them to check what they are actually going to receive, the color, the fit, the texture, the scale. An AI-generated image undermines exactly that check, because the buyer can no longer trust that the thing in the picture is the thing in the box.

Baymard's recommendation follows from that: keep authentic photography for the primary product views, and limit AI-generated visuals to supporting content. AI still has a place, in backgrounds and lifestyle scenes, but only where it does not ask the buyer to take the product itself on faith.

Why it matters for you

If you shoot products for clients, or want to, this study is your pitch. A client does not buy photography because it looks nice. They buy it because it protects the sale, and now there is a number for that: most of their customers trust a real photograph more, and one in six will leave over a generated one.

Shoot the honest frame. Get the color right, show the texture, give the buyer a sense of scale. Save the generated backgrounds for the supporting shots, never the hero image. The camera you already own does the one thing AI cannot: it proves the product exists.
