An Award Only for Photographers Under Three Years In. These Winners Are Worth Studying

October 15, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: The Association of Photographers has announced its 2026 Emerging Talent Awards, open only to photographers practicing less than three years. Gold went to Austin Bradley for a 57-metre highline rigged across a shrinking Icelandic glacier; silver to Ritchie Elder for a documentary series on a Ukrainian Saturday school in Scotland.

Who won, and what did they make?

The AOP, the U.K.-based Association of Photographers, runs its Emerging Talent Awards for what it calls the new wave: photographers who have been practicing their craft for less than three years. There are no restrictions on what can be entered, and it is open to members and non-members alike.

Gold went to Austin Bradley for 57 Metres: Iceland's Shrinking Glacier. Bradley rigged a 57-metre-high line across Iceland's Sólheimajökull Glacier, the length chosen to represent roughly how far the glacier retreats each year as temperatures rise. Working over several days with highliners, a small film crew, and a glaciologist, he turned an abstract climate statistic into a visual you can stand inside. The winning images will be exhibited on billboards across London from October 19 to 25.

Silver went to Ritchie Elder for Saturday School, documenting the final day of term at the Ukrainian Saturday School at Leith Academy, the first school of its kind in Scotland. It gives Ukrainian children displaced by the war a place to learn their language, culture, and history. Elder worked closely with the children, parents, and staff to build the series, which is part of a wider documentary project on Ukrainian community life across Scotland.

What is the lesson for a new photographer?

Neither winner won on technique alone. They won on commitment to an idea. Bradley found a way to make glacial retreat visible at human scale. Elder spent real time inside a community and earned the access that makes documentary work honest. Both projects took days, collaborators, and planning. The camera was the last step, not the first.

That is the pattern worth copying. New photographers often assume they are competing on sharpness, bokeh, or gear. Judges at this level are rewarding something else: a clear concept, pursued properly, finished completely. Pick one subject. Go deeper than feels reasonable. Bring other people into the making of it.

Why it matters for you

If you have been shooting for less than three years, this competition was built for you, literally. The entry rules say so. Most learners wait years before entering anything, assuming they need permission that arrives with experience. The AOP just put work by sub-three-year photographers on billboards across London.

Your inexperience is not the barrier you think it is. The judges told you what they reward: an idea with a spine, carried through to the end. Choose one project, give it the weeks it deserves, and enter it somewhere that takes new work seriously. The next winners list has room for a name like yours.
