The Nikon D850 Is End of Life. That Does Not Make It Dead

November 28, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: Nikon Sales Support in the UK has told retailers the D850 is now end of life, with no further stock expected and no new orders accepted. That is a regional supply decision, not the end of the camera: US retailers still sell it new, Japan still has stock, and the used market is deep.

What exactly did Nikon say?

The wording comes from Nikon itself. Nikon Rumors published the message from Nikon Sales Support on November 26: the D850 model is now end of life, no further stock is expected, new orders are not being accepted, and retailers should update their systems accordingly. The UK shelf already reflects it. Wex, a major UK photo retailer, no longer lists the D850, and another retailer is down to a few lens kits.

That is the whole announcement. No global discontinuation notice, no final production date, no statement about service or parts. One region's sales team has closed the order book.

Is the D850 discontinued everywhere?

Not yet, and the gap matters. PetaPixel checked the other side of the map: the D850 remains available in Japan, and in the United States B&H still carries it new, discounted by $300 through November 29, while Nikon USA still sells it directly. PetaPixel also names the two caveats that apply to almost every discontinuation story. Cameras are often discontinued in one region before another, and production may have halted long before local stock ran dry, so the UK notice may record a decision made a while ago rather than make one now.

PetaPixel has asked Nikon for clarification and will update when it arrives. Until then, the honest summary is narrow: new stock in the UK is finished, new stock elsewhere is not, and brand-new availability will end everywhere eventually, as it does for every camera.

What should a buyer actually check?

Not the production status. What decides whether a discontinued camera is a good buy is what was true the day before the notice: shutter count on a used body, whether batteries and cards are still easy to get, whether repair shops still service it, and whether the lenses you want exist at prices you accept. The D850 passes most of those tests easily today, which is why PetaPixel concludes it will stay easy to buy for the foreseeable future, new for a while and used for long after.

The risk to avoid is the emotional one: panic-buying a body you do not need because the order book closed, or panic-selling a working camera because a sales email made it sound obsolete. Neither reaction changes a single photograph the camera will take tomorrow.

Why it matters for you

Every camera you own will get this notice one day, including the mirrorless body you shoot with now. When it comes, the email will be about stock and systems, not about your pictures. A camera is dead when it cannot be powered, fed with cards, or repaired at a sane price, and not one day before. Read end of life the way retailers do, as a supply signal: buy the tool for the work it does today, check the support around it, and let somebody else pay the panic premium.
