Why Your Zoom Lens Loses Focus When You Zoom

November 19, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: Most photo zooms are varifocal: change the focal length and focus shifts with it. A Lensrentals bench test measured an average 0.374mm of focus drift on a Nikon 70-200mm f/4, against a cinema zoom's 0.008mm. Autofocus corrects it instantly, so you only notice when you focus by hand.

What does parfocal actually mean?

A parfocal lens holds focus while its focal length changes. Focus on a face at the long end, zoom back to wide, and the face is still sharp. A varifocal lens does not behave that way: change its focal length and the focused plane slides, so you have to focus again.

Lensrentals put the numbers on it. Across ten copies of the Nikon 70-200mm f/4 VR, the average change in best-focus position from 70mm to 200mm was 0.374mm. The same test on a Zeiss 70-200mm T2.9 cinema zoom averaged 0.008mm.

Why doesn't your photo zoom do it?

Holding the image still while the zoom group moves takes a second moving group, the compensator, whose only job is to cancel the image-plane travel the zooming creates. Cinema zooms spend real money on that tolerance, and the compensator is adjustable by a technician. Photo zooms have nothing like it, because designers know autofocus covers for the drift.

When does it actually bite you?

Autofocus hides the problem right up to the moment you ask the lens to do something the autofocus system is not watching. A push-in during a take, shot on manual focus, starts sharp and ends soft. The old habit of zooming in to nail focus and zooming back out to compose is precisely the operation a varifocal lens punishes.

How do you test the copy in your hands?

The test takes about ten minutes and needs a tripod, a target with fine detail, and live view magnification. Lock the camera down at your normal working distance, open the aperture all the way, zoom to the long end, punch in to maximum magnification, and set focus manually. Then zoom to the wide end without touching the focus ring, punch in again, and look. Finish by shooting a frame at each end and comparing them at 100 percent.

Why it matters for you

Most of the time this is a fact you can safely ignore, because autofocus is doing the work. But the moment you step into video, manual focus, astro, or focus stacking, the drift becomes yours to manage. Focus at the focal length you will shoot at, never zoom after focusing by hand, and test your own copy instead of trusting a forum thread. One copy on one body satisfied one person. Yours is the only verdict that counts.
