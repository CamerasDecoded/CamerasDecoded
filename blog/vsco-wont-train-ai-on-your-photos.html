VSCO Says It Plainly: Your Photos Will Not Train Its AI

November 12, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: VSCO has rewritten its Creator Content Standards to state plainly that it does not use your photos, public or private, paid or free, to train generative AI models. The change follows criticism in May over broad terms wording, and the company says its outside AI providers are held to the same line.

What was wrong with the old wording?

In May, photographers noticed that VSCO's Terms of Use granted the company the right to use the name, image, voice, or likeness of anyone in user content, and to use certain content to develop, train, and improve AI or machine learning models. The company quickly said it would not train AI models on user content, but a spoken reassurance is not a policy, and photographers kept asking for it in writing.

VSCO has now taken that extra step. Its updated Creator Content Standards state the position explicitly, and CEO Eric Wittman wrote to users directly: sharing your work should not mean wondering whether it will be used to train a system designed to generate content without you.

What does the new policy actually say?

The updated standard reads: whether you are a paid member or a non-paying creator, your public or private content will not be used to develop, train, or improve AI or machine learning models for generative AI use. The same protection applies whether you pay or not. That last point matters, because free tiers are usually where protections get thin.

Wittman also draws a distinction photographers should learn to make everywhere. VSCO still uses machine learning and non-generative AI across the platform, to help people find work, search, connect, and edit. That is different, he argues, from using your photographs to train models that generate new images or video. The updated standards make that boundary explicit, and VSCO says its outside AI providers have already agreed not to use content submitted through its services to train generative models.

Why it matters for you

Your photographs are not just files. They are the hours you spent learning, the places you went, and the people who trusted you in front of the lens. Every platform you post to has a policy about what it can do with that work, and the honest ones will state it in one plain sentence you do not need a lawyer to read. VSCO just did. Before you upload your best work anywhere, find that sentence. If a platform cannot give you one, that is your answer.
