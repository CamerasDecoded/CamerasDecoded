Sirui's New $499 and $599 Fast Primes Make Wide-Angle Affordable Again

November 10, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: Sirui has added a 15mm f/1.6 for $599 and a 24mm f/1.4 for $499 to its Aurora line of autofocus full-frame primes, for Sony E-mount and L-mount. Both have stepper-motor autofocus, close focusing, aperture rings, and weather sealing, bringing fast wide glass down to a price a learner can actually reach.

What did Sirui announce?

Two full-frame autofocus primes in the Aurora series: a 15mm f/1.6 and a 24mm f/1.4. The 15mm focuses as close as 0.3 metres and takes 77mm filters. The 24mm focuses to 0.26 metres and takes 62mm filters. Both weigh around 550 grams and carry a physical aperture ring that can be clicked or declicked, a customizable function button, an autofocus switch, a USB-C port for firmware updates, and a dust and splash resistant metal build. Both are on sale now.

Why is L-mount the interesting part?

Wide-angle autofocus primes exist for L-mount from Panasonic, Sigma, and Viltrox, but few have been this fast at this price. For E-mount there are more comparable options, but more competition is welcome there too. The gap matters because L-mount owners, including Panasonic and Leica shooters, have had fewer affordable ways into fast wide glass.

What does a fast wide prime teach you?

A zoom lets you stand still. A wide prime makes you move your feet, and that movement is the lesson. At 24mm you learn to step closer until the foreground has weight. At 15mm you learn to keep horizons level and edges honest, because the lens exaggerates everything. The f/1.4 and f/1.6 apertures let you shoot in low light and separate a subject from its background without reaching for a longer lens.

Why it matters for you

A prime at this price is a practice tool, not a trophy. If you shoot landscapes, interiors, street, or astro, a fast wide prime changes what you can attempt after dark and how close you can work. The 24mm is the everyday wide to start with. The 15mm is the specialist for big scenes and tight rooms. Either way, the lesson is the same: the lens that makes you move is the lens that teaches you to see.
