Seven Years on Mirrorless, One Week Back on a DSLR

November 24, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: After seven years shooting mirrorless, DPReview's Mitchell Clark spent a week with a Nikon D7500 DSLR and came back with a sharper sense of what mirrorless actually gives you: a live exposure preview, autofocus that tracks without thinking, and a tilt screen that makes low angles easy.

What happens when a mirrorless shooter goes back to a DSLR?

In February 2020, DPReview's Mitchell Clark sold his Nikon D500 and bought a Fujifilm X-T3, joining the mirrorless move. He has not regularly used a DSLR since. This month he borrowed a friend's Nikon D7500, a late-era enthusiast DSLR, and shot with it for a week to find out what, if anything, he had been missing.

The answer, in short: not much, and the things he missed were not the ones he expected.

What did mirrorless quietly take over?

Exposure, first. The electronic viewfinder shows you the picture before you take it, and Clark found himself taking accurate exposure previews for granted. On the D7500 he watched the meter carefully and still came home with high-contrast frames that missed. The mirrorless preview is not a convenience. It is a teacher that shows you the result of every setting in real time.

Autofocus, second. The D7500 has 3D Tracking, but it covers only part of the frame, and its face detection works only in live view, where autofocus is weaker and there is no continuous mode. Getting sharp images took constant attention to the autofocus system, attention taken straight from composition. Modern mirrorless autofocus does not just hit more often. It frees your mind for the picture.

And framing. Years of mirrorless had trained Clark to shoot from odd angles with the tilt screen. On the DSLR, low and high angles meant contortion or guesswork, since live view on most DSLRs was always a compromise, not a mode.

Is there anything the DSLR still does better?

Yes, and it is worth naming honestly. The optical viewfinder lets you look through the lens with the camera switched off, a way to scout a scene before committing to it. Battery life trounces any mirrorless camera: Clark shot for a week without thinking about charging. And the physical shutter gives a satisfying, definite click that no electronic shutter imitates.

Clark had even been entertaining buying a used D500 for about $800. The week with the D7500 cured him of it. The rose tint was gone.

Why it matters for you

If you learned on mirrorless, you may never have noticed what your camera is doing for you. The exposure preview is the fastest exposure teacher ever built into a camera: change a setting, watch the picture change, learn the connection in seconds. The autofocus that tracks a subject across the frame is not laziness. It is the camera handling the mechanics so you can handle the seeing. Use those tools deliberately: turn the level indicator on, watch the histogram in the viewfinder, and let the camera show you what it is about to record.
