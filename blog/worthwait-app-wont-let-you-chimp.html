The App That Won't Let You Chimp

October 22, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: Documentary photographer Dennis Berti built WorthWait, an iPhone app that recreates the disposable-camera experience: 24 or 36 shots per roll, a fixed 35mm lens, and a mandatory 24-hour wait before you can see your photos. The delay is deliberate. It trains you to trust your eye and your settings instead of the screen on the back of the camera.

What does the app actually do?

WorthWait recreates the disposable-camera experience on your phone. You load a roll of 24 or 36 exposures, you are locked to a 35mm-equivalent lens, and once you finish shooting, you wait 24 hours before the photos develop. There is no peeking. Four simulated films are included — Original, Negative, Positive, and Flat — with aspect ratios from 1:1 to 4:3.

The free version is nearly complete: one roll at a time, all four film looks, and Blind Mode. The $14.99 paid version adds bigger rolls and multiple rolls at once, plus the option to develop instantly — which defeats the entire purpose. WorthWait is on iOS now and comes to Android in November.

What is Blind Mode?

Blind Mode removes the viewfinder entirely. You raise the phone and shoot with no preview at all — 'just the light, your instinct, and where your hand is pointing,' as Berti puts it. You will not know what the frame contains until the roll develops a full day later. It is the most honest version of the app's argument: the picture was taken when you pressed the shutter, not when you approved it on a screen.

Why does the wait matter?

Most of us have a habit photographers call chimping: checking the back screen after every shot. Every glance breaks your attention. You stop reading the scene and start auditioning frames, and the camera is a great tool for exposing a photo but a terrible one for judging it, because on the back screen you judge with your anxiety, not your eyes.

A 24-hour gap separates the act of seeing from the act of judging. By the time you look, the moment is gone and only the photograph remains. That gap is where taste grows.

Why it matters for you

You do not need this app to learn the lesson. Try one outing with the habit broken: set your camera to show no preview after the shot, and review at home. The skill being trained is not nostalgia. It is trust — in your eye, your settings, and your ability to make the picture before you see it.
