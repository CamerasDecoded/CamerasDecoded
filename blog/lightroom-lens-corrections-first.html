Turn On Lens Corrections Before You Touch a Slider

November 18, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: Enable lens profile corrections and chromatic aberration removal before any other edit in Lightroom. They fix distortion, dark corners, and color fringes at the file level, so every slider after them works on a clean image. If you shoot adapted lenses, check the profile Lightroom picked, because it guesses from EXIF data and it guesses wrong.

What does a lens profile actually fix?

Every lens bends straight lines, darkens its corners, or leaves thin colored fringes along high-contrast edges to some degree. Lightroom knows the optical fingerprint of most lenses and can correct the worst of it with one checkbox, before you move a single exposure slider.

That order matters. If you skip the correction, every adjustment afterward sits on top of a slightly warped, unevenly lit file, and you end up compensating for distortion with exposure and color moves that would never have been needed.

Why would Lightroom pick the wrong lens?

Lightroom picks a profile by reading the EXIF data in your file, and that chain breaks when an adapter sits between the lens and the body. Photographer Christian Mohrle ran into exactly that with an autumn lake scene shot on a Canon 16-35mm lens: enabling profile corrections changed nothing at first, because Lightroom had guessed the wrong model.

He set the brand and picked the correct lens from the list by hand. Once he did, the distortion flattened out and the dark corners lifted with essentially one click. If you shoot adapted glass and assumed your corrections were working, open that panel on a few recent files and check what Lightroom actually selected.

What comes after the correction?

With the geometry sorted, the edit starts in the Basic panel: a stronger color profile, highlights pulled down, shadows and blacks lifted, then contrast added back so the file does not go flat. Chromatic aberration removal stays on for every image, no exceptions, because the green fringe on a high-contrast edge is invisible at normal viewing size until you zoom in.

The lens sliders are creative controls too. Mohrle pushed distortion further than the profile required because he liked the stretched look, and nudged vignetting the other way to brighten the corners. Corrections first, taste second.

Why it matters for you

Editing order compounds. A thirty-second habit, profile corrections and chromatic aberration on before anything else, means every slider you touch afterward is working on the image you actually shot. Skip it and you will spend the edit fixing problems that were never in the scene, only in the glass. Check the panel on your next import, especially if any of your lenses sit on an adapter.
