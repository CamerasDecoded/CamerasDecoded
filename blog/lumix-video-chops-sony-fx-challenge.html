Lumix Has the Video Chops to Beat Sony. So Why Won't It Try?

November 19, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: Lumix makes some of the most capable video cameras you can buy, but it has never answered Sony's FX3 with a camera built for the same job. In its 25th anniversary year it launched the SHX1 box camera instead. The lesson for buyers is blunt: capability alone does not win work. The camera people recognize does.

What did Lumix actually launch?

This year is the 25th anniversary of the Lumix brand, and Panasonic opened it by saying it had more planned for 2026. The year ends with the SHX1, a box-style cinema camera with 6K open gate recording and Blackmagic RAW to an SSD. There is nothing wrong with the SHX1. It is an excellent camera that will resonate with current Lumix filmmakers. It is also not the camera that caps a celebratory year.

Why does the FX3 own the set?

Sony announced the FX3 in 2021 and in one move created the perfect camera for the mid- to high-end filmmaker at a sub-$4,000 price. It became the industry standard for event and commercial work, and the reason is not only what it does. The FX3 became the visual language of a semi-professional small camera. When producers see that gray body with the sharp-edged grip, they immediately know it is acceptable.

Is Lumix actually behind on video?

No, and that is what makes the story frustrating rather than simple. Lumix's in-body stabilization is better. Its record modes are, on the whole, better. The company was giving filmmakers open gate and in-camera anamorphic desqueeze years before it was even a twinkle in the other brands' eyes. Canon and Nikon got the design memo, which is why the ZR and the R6V wear the same boxy language. The S1 II is every bit as capable, but it looks like an old DSLR. Sony even stumbled over the FX5 launch, handing Lumix the perfect window, and Lumix was not prepared for the moment.

Why it matters for you

If you are choosing a system for paid video work, the lesson is uncomfortable but useful: buy the camera that gets you hired, not just the one with the best spec sheet. A producer's comfort with a recognizable tool is part of the job, and no amount of open gate changes that. And if you already shoot Lumix, take the other half of the lesson. Your footage is not the problem. The fight Lumix is losing is about visibility, not image quality, and visibility is a fight you can win yourself, one delivered job at a time.
