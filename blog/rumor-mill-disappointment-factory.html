Do Not Let a Camera That Does Not Exist Disappoint You

November 20, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: DPReview's Mitchell Clark argues the rumor mill manufactures disappointment: leaks and speculation inflate expectations until any real camera looks like a failure. Panasonic's SHX1 was supposed to carry an unprecedented sensor with 8K and a global shutter. It arrived with the S1II's sensor, rebuilt for video work, which is a solid camera judged on its own terms.

What did the rumor mill promise this time?

Panasonic's new box camera, the SHX1, was announced this week, and if you had been following the Lumix subreddit or the rumor sites, you might feel a little let down by it. The speculation had it carrying an unprecedented sensor, maybe one that could shoot 8K, using the organic sensor technology Panasonic has worked on for over a decade to unlock a global shutter and variable ND, or a LOFIC design for extraordinary dynamic range.

None of that was ever announced. It was assembled from a few real leaked specs, misinformation, and optimistic expectation, then extrapolated out to the moon. That is how the rumor mill works.

What did the real camera turn out to be?

The SHX1 is, essentially, an S1II designed to be used solely by video professionals. It has the same sensor as the S1II, which is fine: there currently are not that many sensors out there that are better for video. It has a few new features, some of them pretty cool, but it will not suddenly unlock ways of shooting that were unthinkable with Panasonic's hybrid cameras.

That is what a camera release in 2026 mostly looks like: the same parts, rearranged to better fit a specific audience. Clark's verdict is that this is a good thing, and he is not especially disappointed. The disappointment belongs to the expectations, not the product.

Why does the cycle keep repeating?

This scene plays out before pretty much every camera launch. A few real specs leak, people extrapolate, and if the real camera does not match what a commenter imagined, it becomes a crushing disappointment, sometimes even a reason to declare a brand switch. Clark calls it an unhealthy way to think about cameras, and the language makes it worse: people use the words confirmed and official for rumors, which is literally not the case.

Why it matters for you

Cameras are complex machines, and while a spec sheet sometimes tells you how one will perform, there are just as many cameras that end up being more than the sum of their parts. But to tell those apart, you need to actually know what the camera is first, and the rumor mill never lets you get there. Judge a camera by the work it produces, not by the camera it was supposed to be.
