The Adobe MAX Updates That Matter Have Nothing to Do With AI

November 11, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: Adobe's November 2026 updates to Camera Raw and Lightroom add the tools learners actually use every day: virtual copies for trying different edits without duplicating files, automatic stacks that tidy burst sequences, Denoise that now works on JPEGs as well as Raws, and LUT support for color grading. The headline AI tools got the keynote; these got the work done.

What did Adobe actually ship?

Camera Raw 19, released alongside the MAX announcements, quietly adds the kind of features that change how you work rather than how you talk about your work. Its Glow tool now adds motion, which streaks light the way a shutter drag would, and streak, which mimics the look of shooting point lights with an anamorphic lens. Both are adjustable, so they read as a choice rather than a filter.

The practical upgrades matter more. Denoise, once limited to Raw files, now works on JPEGs, HEIFs, PNGs, and WebP files, including inside Photoshop. Camera Raw also supports LUTs for color grading, stackable up to three at a time with adjustable strength, plus virtual copies, so you can try different edits on the same photo without duplicating the file. Burst sequences from recent Canon, Nikon, and DNG-supported cameras are now grouped into automatic stacks, and you can temporarily hide photos from the filmstrip to keep your view clean.

What changed in Lightroom?

Lightroom's cloud version gets a more customizable interface, with first-open presets for new, power, and longtime users, plus a custom panel of favorite edit controls. Assisted Culling is more accurate at picking keepers, you can save multiple versions of a photo whether it lives locally or in the cloud, and white balance adjustments have more range.

Lightroom Classic gains import filtering by color label, file type, or locked status, and the autofocus point overlay from Camera Raw for selected cameras. Both versions get a vectorscope for judging color, a projection adjustment for fixing wide-angle distortion, and Depth Range Masking that can now generate a depth map for photos that do not already have one.

Why it matters for you

The lesson is about where to spend your learning time. The AI announcements make headlines, but the organizational tools make your editing faster and braver: virtual copies let you experiment without fear, stacks tame the burst sequences every mirrorless camera now produces, and Denoise on JPEGs rescues older files and phone shots you thought were finished. Learn these first, and the AI buttons become a shortcut for work you already understand, not a substitute for it.
