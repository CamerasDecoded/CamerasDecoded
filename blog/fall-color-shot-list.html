Twenty Years of Fall Color, One Shot List

October 4, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: After twenty years photographing fall color, Kate Garibaldi's biggest lesson is simple: arrive with a shot list. Peak color lasts only a few weeks, and a list of shot types, big vistas, waterfalls, macro leaves, wildlife, barns, gives each outing direction without dictating the photograph. Variety is what builds a portfolio instead of folders of the same landscape.

What goes on a fall color shot list?

The list is a menu of shot types for the whole season, not one photograph planned in advance: big panoramic vistas, waterfalls with rocks for leading lines in a long exposure, environmental wildlife where the animal sits small in a larger autumn landscape, tighter wildlife portraits against foliage, macro photographs of leaves, textures like moss and bark that contrast with bright colors, barns and covered bridges, and abstracts or reflections that do not look like traditional fall landscapes. One outing might produce waterfall, macro, and texture images; another might be wildlife and vistas. The list keeps her shooting what is missing from her collection, not whatever is easiest.

How does weather decide what you shoot?

She stops asking whether the forecast looks good and asks what kind of photography the conditions suit. Overcast days are favorites for waterfalls and macro: the clouds act like a giant softbox, killing harsh contrast so bright highlights hold detail. Recent rain makes waterfalls better still, with more flow, saturated wet rocks, and puddle reflections. For big vistas she wants the opposite: partly cloudy weather around sunrise or sunset, with dramatic clouds and warm light. The weather never picks the exact photograph. It picks which kinds of photographs are worth pursuing that day.

Why it matters for you

Peak color is a window of a few weeks, and it is open right now. Borrow the shot list: write down eight types of photographs you want this season, check the forecast the night before, and let the conditions choose the type. Overcast? Skip the vista and find a waterfall. Golden light promised at sunset? Get to the overlook. Then make the famous view once, and work the rest of the list: lone trees, trails as leading lines, stream reflections, the forest floor. Variety is a choice you make before you leave home.
