Sigma's 50-120mm f/2.8 Is the APS-C Zoom We've Been Waiting For

October 8, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: Sigma has officially announced the 50-120mm f/2.8 DC OS Contemporary for APS-C cameras: a 390-gram, weather-sealed telephoto zoom priced at $799. It completes Sigma's affordable f/2.8 trio and finally gives crop-sensor shooters the portrait-and-events zoom that full-frame photographers have taken for granted for decades.

What does 50-120mm actually cover?

On an APS-C sensor, 50-120mm behaves like 75-180mm on full frame. That is the classic working range for portraits, events, and sideline sports: long enough to compress features flatteringly and isolate a subject from a busy background, short enough to stay mobile. One zoom replaces the 50mm and 85mm primes most learners juggle, and it keeps f/2.8 open at every focal length. It launches for Sony E, Canon RF, and Fujifilm X mounts, so every major APS-C system gets it.

Why does a constant f/2.8 matter for learning?

A constant aperture removes a variable from the exposure triangle while you learn. At f/2.8 wide open, indoor receptions and evening sidelines stay shootable without cranking the ISO into mush, and the background separation teaches you what compression and depth of field actually do to a scene. The depth of field behaves more like f/4 on full frame, which is plenty for portraits, and Sigma included optical stabilization plus a proper aperture ring. Reviewers found focus fast enough for sports and street work, with clean, round bokeh and no distracting fringing.

Where does it compromise?

Compact and $799 means tradeoffs. Bright light sources can cause a veiled flare that washes out contrast, though some portrait shooters will like that glow in backlit frames, and stopping down tames it. Corner sharpness is soft wide open, especially at the 120mm end, and cleans up a couple of stops down. Sigma tested it on a 40-megapixel Fujifilm X-E5, which shows softness more brutally than the 24-megapixel bodies most learners carry, so on your camera it will look better than the lab charts suggest.

Why it matters for you

Sigma already makes the 10-18mm f/2.8 and the 18-50mm f/2.8. Add this lens and you own the entire pro zoom trinity for under a kilogram combined, at a total cost below one full-frame 70-200mm f/2.8. The deeper lesson is about carrying: the lens you take everywhere is the lens you learn on, and a 390-gram zoom goes to the sidelines, the reception, and the park in a way a two-kilo pro lens never will. APS-C's old excuse, that the format never gets serious glass, just lost its best example.
