Fujifilm Just Raised Prices Again. Here Is What It Means for Your Next Camera

October 1, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: Fujifilm raised Japanese prices up to 23 percent on October 1 — bodies, lenses, and Instax alike, including the X100VI and X-T50. It is the company's second revision in eighteen months, driven by parts and logistics costs. The signal for buyers: prices drift one way, so buy when you are ready, not when the price is perfect.

What went up, and by how much?

As of today, Fujifilm cameras and lenses cost more in Japan. Digital camera bodies are up roughly 6 to 15 percent, one lens — the XF 500mm f/5.6 — is up about 12 percent, and the Instax lineup took the biggest hit at 6 to 23 percent. The GFX100 II, GFX100S II, and GFX100RF medium format bodies all went up. On the X Series side, the increases touch the X-T50, the X-E5, and the X100VI — a camera that still sells out as fast as it restocks, two and a half years after launch.

Why is Fujifilm raising prices?

Fujifilm points to the same pressure every manufacturer is feeling: parts, materials, manufacturing, and logistics all cost more, and efficiency gains stopped covering it. This is the company’s second Japanese price revision in eighteen months — analog products went up by as much as 52 percent in April 2025 — and Europe saw increases of 5 to 11 percent recently. The new prices are Japan-only for now.

Why it matters for you

Camera prices drift in one direction. If you have been waiting for the X100VI or an X-T50 to get cheaper, the signal says the opposite — buy when you are ready, not when the price is perfect, because the perfect price already passed. Budget the whole system, not just the body: lenses and film costs are rising too. And keep an eye on the used market, where yesterday’s prices still live.

The camera you can afford today is the camera you learn on today.
