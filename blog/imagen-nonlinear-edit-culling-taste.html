Culling Is Where Your Taste Gets Trained. Do Not Automate It Away

November 26, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: Imagen's Intelligent Studio collapses culling, AI editing, and manual finishing into one non-linear workspace with a plain-language Assistant and export-based pricing. It is built for high-volume professionals. Learners should borrow its speed selectively, because choosing frames before polishing them is how photographic judgment is trained.

What changed in the workflow?

Order is training, and Imagen just made the order optional. As DPReview reported on November 24, Intelligent Studio removes the fixed cull-then-edit sequence that has defined AI-assisted post-production, letting photographers work in any order inside one application and eliminating the round-trip to Lightroom. The update has been rolling out in stages inside the existing Imagen product since September 29, 2026. The context is volume: a wedding photographer coming off a busy season can face more than 20,000 photos waiting to be worked through.

Two additions carry the change. A full manual editor now lives inside Imagen, covering RAW and JPEG files with Basic adjustments, Curves, HSL across eight color ranges, Point Color, color grading, detail, lens corrections, geometry, effects, calibration, masks, and presets. Edits made there are not synced back as Lightroom-compatible files, and Imagen states plainly that the tool is not positioned as a Lightroom replacement. Pricing moves with the workflow: photographers now pay on export, through Pay-As-You-Go with a $7 monthly minimum plus usage, or a flat Limitless plan, so culling and experimenting on frames that never ship costs nothing.

What does the Assistant actually do?

A plain-language Assistant turns one prompt into a search, a selection, and an adjustment at the same time, across five categories: culling and selection, look and feel, conditional edits, workflow and studio tasks, and diagnostics. The examples DPReview cites are telling. Denoise all images with ISO above 2000. Apply one film profile to outdoor photos and another to indoor groups of more than 10 people. The value is translation, not magic. The photographer already knows which frames need what. The Assistant lets them say it instead of clicking through it frame by frame.

What comes next is still a roadmap, and it should be read that way. Imagen describes future work toward AI that anticipates a photographer's eye, real-time profile application, a proprietary Editor model that learns from work inside the app, and filters such as key-people detection and indoor or outdoor recognition. None of that is the shipped product today. The shipped product is a faster way to execute decisions you have already made.

Why it matters for you

If you are learning, keep one part of the old order on purpose. Cull by eye, without prompts, often enough that choosing stays a skill and not a setting. Looking at 200 frames and defending the 12 that survive is how you learn what your camera missed, what your timing cost you, and what you actually like. Use non-linear tools where they help most: batch denoise, consistent color across a set, and finishing work after the selects are yours. Speed is a professional virtue. Judgment is the product. The photographers who will use tools like this best are the ones who can still do the slow version well, because the prompt can only express a taste that already exists.
