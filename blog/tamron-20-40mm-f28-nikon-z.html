Tamron's 20-40mm f/2.8 Comes to Nikon Z

October 9, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: Tamron is bringing its full-frame 20-40mm f/2.8 Di III VXD zoom to Nikon Z mount, shipping October 22 for $729. At 380 grams with a constant f/2.8 aperture, it gives Nikon shooters a compact, fast wide-to-normal zoom — a range no other Z-mount lens currently covers in a single barrel.

What's unusual about 20-40mm?

Tamron has a long tradition of odd focal lengths, and 20-40mm is one of its oddest. On full frame it runs from genuine ultrawide to a normal lens in one barrel: 20mm for architecture, interiors, and big landscapes; 40mm for environmental portraits, street, and everyday walk-around shooting. DPReview notes there is no direct Z-mount competitor covering that exact span — other ultrawide zooms either start wider and end earlier, or start tighter and reach further.

The constraint is the lesson. A limited-range zoom forces you to move your feet instead of standing still and twisting the ring, which is how beginners learn to see compositions before they raise the camera. And it pairs cleanly with the rest of Tamron's lineup: the 12-20mm f/2.8 goes wider, the 35-100mm f/2.8 or 35-150mm f/2-2.8 goes longer, with no gaps between them.

What do you get for $729?

Optically the lens is unchanged from the 2022 Sony E version: 12 elements in 11 groups with four low-dispersion elements and three aspherics, nine rounded aperture blades, close focus down to 17cm for 1:3.8 reproduction, and dust- and moisture-resistant sealing with a 67mm filter thread. The VXD linear autofocus motors are fast and quiet, which Tamron pitches at video work — the range suits vlogging and gimbal shooting.

The Z version is 89mm long and weighs 380 grams, light enough to leave mounted all day. It costs $30 more than the $699 Sony version, but that's still less than buying the E-mount lens plus an adapter, and the native mount gives a more consistent experience.

Why it matters for you

If you shoot Nikon Z and have been waiting for affordable fast wide glass, this fills the gap at a fraction of the cost and weight of Nikon's own pro zooms. As a learning tool it's arguably more valuable than a spec sheet suggests: one light, fast wide zoom that you actually carry will teach you more about wide-angle composition than any lens you leave at home.
