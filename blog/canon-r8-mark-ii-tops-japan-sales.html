A $1,899 Canon Just Ended Sony's Nine-Month Run at the Top in Japan

November 10, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: The Canon EOS R8 Mark II became Japan's best-selling camera in October 2026, weeks after it started shipping, ending the Sony A7 V's nine-month run at the top of Map Camera's chart. At $1,899 it pairs a 24.2MP full-frame sensor with in-body stabilization, 40fps bursts, and 4K60 video, and buyers loved it with the RF 45mm f/1.2 STM kit lens.

What happened in Japan?

Map Camera, one of Japan's biggest retailers, published its October 2026 top ten, and the Canon EOS R8 Mark II took first place. The Sony A7 V, which had led the chart nine times since last December, dropped to second. The Canon was only announced in mid-September and started shipping toward the end of October, so it reached the top within weeks of going on sale. Map Camera called the result a game-changer for Canon after a long struggle.

What do you get for $1,899?

A 24.2MP full-frame sensor, in-body stabilization, a joystick and tilt screen, 40fps bursts with Dual Pixel II autofocus, and uncropped 4K video at up to 60 frames per second, oversampled from 6K footage. It keeps the sensor and processor of the original R8 while fixing that camera's biggest complaints, chiefly the lack of stabilization. The original R8 cost $1,649, so the Mark II asks $250 more for a much more complete camera.

Why did it sell so fast?

Map Camera pointed to portability and price matching demand for street photography during the autumn travel season. The kit with the compact RF 45mm f/1.2 STM prime sold at nearly the same pace as the body alone. That is the telling detail. Buyers did not just want a cheap full-frame body. They wanted a small, light kit they would actually carry.

Why it matters for you

Charts are not buying advice, but this one is a useful mirror. The camera that won was not the most powerful or the most expensive. It was the one that removed the most friction between seeing something and shooting it: stabilization for slow light, a joystick for fast focus, a body light enough to carry all day, and a price a learner can reach. When you choose your next camera, weigh those same things. The best camera for learning is the one that is with you, and this result says a lot of photographers are making exactly that choice.
