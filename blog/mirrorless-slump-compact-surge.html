A 7.9 Percent Dip Is Not the End of Mirrorless

November 3, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: Mirrorless shipments are down 7.9 percent in the first nine months of 2026, but the comparison is 2025, the industry's best year since 2019, and total shipments still beat 2024. Compacts are up 15.4 percent to about 1.9 million units. The story is a strong year normalizing and a pocketable second camera finding buyers, not mirrorless cameras losing their place.

What do the numbers actually say?

The figures come from the Camera and Imaging Products Association, CIPA, which tracks monthly production and shipments from Japan's biggest camera makers. Its September 2026 release compares the first nine months of 2026 with the same period in 2025. Overall shipments sit at 95.7 percent of last year's total, and interchangeable-lens cameras at 89.2 percent.

Inside that total, mirrorless shipments are at 92.1 percent of last year's figure, a 7.9 percent drop, with more than 4.1 million mirrorless bodies shipped so far in 2026. DSLRs, the shrinking tail of the market, are at 64.5 percent. Compacts are the exception: up 15.4 percent to about 1.9 million units shipped. Production tells a similar story, with compact production up 21.3 percent while mirrorless production fell 8.3 percent. Lens shipments are at 93.3 percent of last year's total, but their value grew 3.1 percent, which suggests buyers are spending more per lens even as unit counts ease.

Why is the comparison year doing the work?

A percentage against last year only means something if you know what last year was. 2025 was the camera industry's best year since 2019, so 2026 is slowing from a high bar, not falling off a cliff. Total shipments in 2026 still beat 2024's count. September itself was better than August for mirrorless and DSLR shipments, while compacts dipped slightly month to month.

Two caveats keep the picture honest. Camera brands have warned investors about supply shortages, so some of the dip may be units that could not be built rather than demand that vanished. And CIPA covers participating Japanese makers only, which excludes Leica and Hasselblad as well as action-camera makers DJI, GoPro, and Insta360, so this is a picture of the mainstream Japanese industry, not the whole market.

Why it matters for you

Do not switch systems on a headline. A shipment chart is not an image-quality chart, and your mirrorless body did not get worse because fewer of them shipped this year. The camera you are learning on is the same camera it was last month.

If the compact surge tempts you, treat it as what it is: a second camera for the pocket, for the walk, for the moments when the big body stays home. That is a genuinely useful addition to a photographer's kit. It is not a reason to abandon the system you are building skill in. Buy the compact because it goes where your mirrorless does not, not because a chart told you mirrorless is finished.
