Three-Year-Old Cameras Are Outselling the New Ones

November 17, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: At Japanese retailer Kitamura, October's best-selling mirrorless cameras were the three-year-old Sony A7C II and A6700, ahead of every 2026 launch. Buyers are choosing compact bodies they can actually carry over the newest spec sheet, and an older mid-tier body often beats a new entry-level one.

What topped the October sales list?

Japanese retailer Kitamura shared its best-selling mirrorless cameras for October, and the list is led by two compact Sony bodies launched in 2023: the full-frame A7C II in first place and the APS-C A6700 in second. Both are three years old, and both outsold every camera launched in 2026.

The first fresh 2026 model appears in a tie for third: Canon's brand-new EOS R8 Mark II, which only began shipping last month, sits level with the Canon EOS R6 Mark II, a mid-tier body launched in 2022 and already replaced by the Mark III. The Sony A7 IV sits in seventh while its newer successor, the A7 V, follows in eighth.

Why are older cameras winning?

The common thread is size. The A7C II packs a full-frame sensor into a compact body, and the A6700 is smaller still, and both arrived as travel-friendly cameras became the trend. The new launches that did crack the top ten are compact too: the OM System PEN in fifth, the Sigma FP2 in ninth, and the Fujifilm X-M5 in tenth.

What should you buy instead of the newest body?

The Canon tie is the clearest buying lesson on the list. The R6 Mark II is a mid-tier camera with dual card slots, longer battery life, faster mechanical shutter bursts, and a half-stop more stabilization than the new entry-level R8 Mark II, whose biggest advantage is that it is lighter. The price gap is almost nothing: a holiday discount puts the R6 Mark II only about $100 more in the US, and in the UK it is about 30 pounds cheaper right now.

That is the trade most buyers should be making. An older camera from one tier up gives you the build, the battery, and the second card slot that protect real shoots, while the newest entry-level body gives you a newer badge and a lighter bag.

Why it matters for you

The market is telling you what the Arena proves every night: the camera does not make the photo. A three-year-old body you carry everywhere will outshoot a brand-new one that stays home. If you are shopping, look one tier up and one generation back before you look at this year's launch.
