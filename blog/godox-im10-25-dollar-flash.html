A $25 Flash Is the Cheapest Photography Lesson You Can Buy

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: The Godox iM10 is a $24.90 manual flash that weighs 23 grams and fits in a pocket. It has four power levels, no TTL, and no bounce, which is exactly why it is a good teacher: with nothing automatic to hide behind, you have to learn what light actually does. A cheap flash you carry everywhere beats an expensive one you leave at home.

What is the iM10, exactly?

The Godox iM10 Mini Flash fits in a pocket and weighs less than an ounce, 23 grams. It costs $24.90 and is available now. It does not pack a huge punch: Godox does not even specify its output, noting only four manual power levels, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, and 1/1. It works well enough indoors but is unlikely to overpower natural light, and Godox pitches it for indoor snapshots and nighttime portraits.

It has no electronic communication or TTL, so it is a universal manual flash. Its single-contact hot shoe lets it work with compact, mirrorless, and film cameras. It has a built-in battery that recharges via USB-C, promises about 350 full-power flashes per charge, and recycles in three seconds. It does not tilt, bounce, or work off-camera.

Why is a manual-only flash a good teacher?

Because there is nothing to hide behind. A TTL flash guesses for you, and when the guess is wrong you learn nothing about why. Four manual power levels force you to think in ratios: one stop more, one stop less, and what each step does to the face in front of you. That is the whole lesson of flash, and it costs twenty-five dollars.

The size matters as much as the price. A flash that weighs 23 grams goes in the pocket and comes out at dinner, at a friend's birthday, on a night walk. A bigger, better flash that lives in a drawer teaches you nothing. Photographers who need more power should look at the $85 Godox TT350II instead. But that is a different job: this one is about learning to see light, starting with a flash small enough never to leave behind.

Why it matters for you

Flash is the skill most self-taught photographers avoid the longest, and the avoidance is usually about the gear, not the light. A twenty-five-dollar manual flash removes the excuse. Put it on your camera, set it to 1/4, take a portrait in a dim room, and look at what changed. Then try 1/8 and 1/2 and compare. In one evening you will have learned more about light than a month of watching tutorials.
