A Client Had to Tell This Photographer She Was Charging Too Little

October 30, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: Photographer Laura BC charged 250 pounds for a music shoot in London, and the artist told her it was nothing: another photographer earned 3,000 pounds for comparable work, and the artist thought her work was better. Underpricing usually comes from never doing the arithmetic or asking the market.

What happened to Laura BC?

In 2013, Laura BC left Spain for London alone. She had no job, no friends there, and no English, and for a stretch she shared a room with 15 people because it was all she could afford. She slept with her camera bag because it was the only valuable thing she had. It took years for her to become a full-time freelance photographer and eventually a creative director, learning largely from scattered tutorials with no mentor.

Shooting for a music artist in London, she charged 250 pounds, a little over 300 dollars. The artist said that was nothing. Another DJ the artist knew was paying a different photographer 3,000 pounds, and the artist thought her photography was much better than that photographer's. She calls it embarrassing that a client had to be the one to tell her to raise her rates.

Why do self-taught photographers underprice?

Because they price in isolation. Without a mentor or a peer group, there is nobody to say a photo is weak, a pitch is premature, or a rate is far below the market. Laura BC describes both directions of that mistake: charging far too little for work that was ready, and pitching major brands before her work was ready for that level. Both come from the same gap, which is no second opinion.

How do you find your floor?

Start with costs, not confidence. The National Press Photographers Association offers a free cost-of-doing-business calculator: enter your gear, insurance, software subscriptions, rent, and the number of days you realistically bill each year, and it gives you the minimum you need to charge just to stay afloat. That number is your floor, not your price.

Then check the market above that floor. Ask the client what they have budgeted before you quote, and ask a working photographer in your genre what a comparable job pays. Remember the quiet risk of a low quote: a brand with a real budget may read it as inexperience and pass.

Why it matters for you

If you are starting to charge, do the two steps before your next quote, not after it. Run the cost calculator so you know the number below which you are paying to work, and ask one working photographer what the job actually pays. Then quote from evidence, and let the work do the rest. Nobody should have to hear from a client that they are worth ten times what they asked.
