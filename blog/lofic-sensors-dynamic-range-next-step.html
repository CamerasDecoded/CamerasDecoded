The Next Sensor Leap Might Be Dynamic Range, Not Megapixels

November 18, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: LOFIC sensors add a small capacitor beside each photodiode to catch overflow charge from bright highlights instead of clipping them. Rumors suggest the design could reach consumer mirrorless cameras in the next year or two, bringing more than 17 stops of dynamic range, which would mean single-exposure HDR straight out of camera.

What is a LOFIC sensor?

LOFIC stands for Lateral Overflow Integration Capacitor. Each photodiode on the sensor gets an extra built-in capacitor sitting next to it. When a photodiode reaches its electron-storage limit during a bright exposure, the excess charge spills into that capacitor instead of clipping, so the highlight keeps detail instead of blowing out to pure white.

It is a hardware answer to a problem photographers usually solve with technique: bracketing exposures, blending frames, or accepting blown highlights. If the sensor itself holds more range, the file holds more of the scene.

Why does the industry need it?

Digital Camera World's Gavin Stoker argues that camera technology has peaked in the eyes of buyers. AI subject recognition, stacked sensors, and global shutters are real advances, but they have not stopped contracting sales, slowing shipments, or long gaps between new cameras. His case is that the industry needs a dramatic change, something that gives photographers a reason to upgrade and buy again.

That framing is debatable, a better autofocus system is a genuine reason to upgrade, but the underlying point stands: megapixel counts stopped being the story years ago. The next number that changes your files may be dynamic range.

Where is LOFIC already?

It is not only a rumor. The DJI Osmo Pocket 4P has already brought LOFIC hardware to the consumer market in its 1-inch-sensor model, and flagship smartphones are exploring the technology too. Industry rumors cited in the article suggest consumer mirrorless cameras, including current Micro Four Thirds models, could receive it next year or the year after.

No specific mirrorless model is named, and that matters. More than 17 stops of dynamic range is a rumored figure, not a spec sheet, and whether manufacturers charge a premium for it is still an open question. Treat the timeline as a rumor and the technology as real.

Why it matters for you

Seventeen stops of range would change the hardest scenes you shoot: sunsets with a bright sky and dark foreground, windows in interior shots, stage light on a dark room, all captured in one frame without bracketing or blending. Smaller sensors would punch above their weight, which is the most interesting part for anyone shooting Micro Four Thirds.

The practical takeaway is about how you judge your next camera. Megapixels sell boxes. Dynamic range changes files. When LOFIC bodies arrive, that number deserves the same attention you give to autofocus and frame rate today.
