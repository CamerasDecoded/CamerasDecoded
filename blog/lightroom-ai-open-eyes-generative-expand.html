The Finish Is a Decision

October 23, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: Adobe added two generative tools to Lightroom on mobile: Open Eyes, which fixes closed eyes with a single tap, and Generative Expand, which invents background beyond your frame. Both are useful — and both cross a line you have to draw yourself, between finishing a photograph and fabricating one.

What did Adobe just add to Lightroom?

Every edit is a decision, and the moment your software starts inventing parts of the photograph, you owe the frame honesty. The finish is a skill. Tools that generate pixels are only as honest as the photographer using them.

Adobe just gave Lightroom on mobile two new generative tools, reported by DIYPhotography. Open Eyes fixes closed eyes with a single tap, drawing on other photos of the same person in your library to match eye color, shape, and size. Generative Expand stretches an image beyond its borders to fit a different aspect ratio. Both target frustrations every photographer knows: the blink that ruins a group shot, the frame too tight for the crop.

When are these tools genuinely useful?

Used well, they are finishing tools. Open Eyes rescues the one frame where everything else worked — the expression, the light, the moment — and it is free in Lightroom mobile. Generative Expand gives a landscape room to breathe as a square post, or a portrait space for a vertical story, without sacrificing the original pixels to a crop.

But each tool comes with a line you must draw yourself. Generative Expand does not recover detail outside the frame; it creates new visual content, and the invented background can hold objects that were never in the actual scene. Even Open Eyes carries a caveat: AI-generated edits may not always look natural or represent the original moment.

Where is the line between finishing and fabricating?

This is where the craft question lives. A retouch that removes a distraction serves the photograph. An edit that invents an eye, or a background that never existed, changes what the photograph claims. The two look identical inside the app. Only your intention separates them.

Why it matters for you

Before you tap any generative tool, decide what the edit is for. If it restores what you saw, it is finishing. If it invents what you did not see, say so — to your client, your audience, and yourself. The photographers who thrive alongside these tools will be the ones whose edits are traceable to a decision, not a shrug.
