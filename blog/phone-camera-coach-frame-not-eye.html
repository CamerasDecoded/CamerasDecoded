Your Phone Can Now Coach Your Framing. It Still Cannot Teach You to See.

October 30, 2026 · 4 min read
In short: Google's Camera Coach, introduced on the Pixel 10 and refined on the Pixel 11 Pro, suggests reframes and details as you shoot. It is a useful drill for a beginner, but not a teacher of photography: it tells you where to stand, not why a frame works.

What does Camera Coach do?

Camera Coach lives inside the phone's camera app. Open it, tap the coach icon, and select a desired look. It analyzes what is in frame and suggests steps based on the scene: sometimes a simple reframe, sometimes a deeper process that highlights a specific detail or subject. You follow each prompt until you press the shutter. Google introduced it on the Pixel 10 series late last year, tapping professional photographers to help teach the system what makes a good photograph, and has refined it on the Pixel 11 Pro.

What did the reviewer actually find?

The reviewer's verdict is mixed in a useful way. As an experienced photographer, he found many suggestions second nature, and some were exactly what he tries to avoid. The coach made its best recommendations with people rather than inanimate objects, and it offered no suggestions to adjust light. His overall judgment is still generous: democratizing photographic knowledge is a net positive, and a phone that teaches fundamentals makes photography more approachable.

What is the difference between a prompt and a photographer's eye?

A prompt is a step. An eye is a reason. Camera Coach can show you a top-down angle or a close detail, but it cannot tell you what you were trying to say, whether the light deserved the frame, or whether the suggested crop flatters the subject or flattens it. Those judgments are the craft, and they are learned by making the call yourself, seeing the result, and adjusting next time.

Use the coach the way you would use any drill: take the suggestion, then ask what changed and whether you would keep it without the prompt. If you can answer that, the skill transfers to any camera, including a mirrorless body where you control the light, the aperture, and the moment yourself.

Why it matters for you

Borrow the coach's best habit, which is pausing before the shutter to consider the frame, and leave its worst habit behind, which is accepting a suggestion without asking why. Try this on your next outing: shoot the scene the coach suggests, then shoot it again the way you would have without it, and compare the two at home. The frame you can defend is the one you learned from.
