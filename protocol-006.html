Why Your Camera Keeps Focusing on the Wrong Thing

And the one setting that fixes it.

006
OPERATOR PROTOCOL

“The groom's reaction — the exact half-second his face cracked open — tack sharp on the flower arrangement three feet behind him. I stared at the back of the camera while the moment evaporated. The camera had done exactly what I asked. I'd just asked wrong.”

The Myth

You've been told more focus points means better focus. It doesn't. A hundred focus points is a hundred chances for the camera to pick the wrong hero — and it will, every time, because it loves contrast and has no idea what a face is for.

The Truth

Autofocus doesn't see subjects. It sees edges — the closest, highest-contrast edge inside whatever area you let it search. Give it the whole frame and it picks the bush. Give it one point and it has no choice but yours. One point. That's the whole fix: a single focus point, placed by you, on the thing that matters.

The Operator's Protocol — Nail Focus in 3 Steps
01
Shrink to one point. Switch to single-point AF. One square, not a zone, not a grid. You are now the only intelligence in the focusing system.
02
Put it on the eye. Move that point onto the nearest eye — or the hero detail — before you think about composition. Focus first, frame second.
03
Lock and recompose. Half-press to lock, then reframe. The focus stays where you put it while you build the shot around it.
Pro tip: For moving subjects, keep the single point but switch the drive to continuous AF — one point that tracks beats a hundred points that wander.
The Payoff

The eye is sharp. The moment is sharp. You stop zooming in at 100% with dread and start doing it with confidence. You didn't need a camera with better autofocus. You needed to stop delegating the only decision that matters.
