Video settings, in depth

What are the right video settings on the R6 II — formats, Canon Log 3, and true slow motion?

camerasdecoded.com/camera-select/canon/eos-r6-ii/video-settings
The answer — share this
4K 59.94 IPB as the default, Canon Log 3 only when the footage will be graded, High Frame Rate for true slow motion — and batteries planned, because this body offers no heat setting to hide behind.
Movie rec. size: 4K 59.94 IPB · Canon Log settings → Canon Log: On (C.LOG3) for graded work · View Assist: On · High Frame Rate: 179.82 fps → 1/6-speed playback
Cameras Decoded — Camera Select
Decision 1

Two compressions, one default

MENU→Shooting→Movie rec. size

Every movie is MP4, compressed as IPB (Standard) or IPB (Light). Standard is the quality default at 4K up to 59.94p — there is no 4K 120p on this body. Light records the same sizes at lower bit rates: smaller files, gentler on cards and laptops, the pick when volume beats the last of the quality.

Decision 2

Log is one switch here

MENU→Shooting→Canon Log settings

Canon Log → On (C.LOG3) records Canon Log 3 in 10-bit 4:2:2 — flat, wide, built to be graded. The manual states the full dynamic range (approx. 1600%) arrives at ISO 800 or higher; ISO 100–640 is expanded and narrower, so do not base log exposure down there. View Assist shows viewable color on screen while the file stays flat. Shipping ungraded? Leave log off.

Decision 3

Slow motion is a Full HD tool

MENU→Shooting→Movie rec. size

High Frame Rate recording reaches 179.82 fps in Full HD, written as a 29.97 fps file — playback is 1/6 speed with the slow motion already in it (119.88 fps writes at 1/4 speed). Costs: no headphone monitoring during HFR, and one clip caps at an hour at 179.82. For 4K, 59.94p is the ceiling.

Decision 4

Plan batteries, not settings

MENU→Shooting→Movie rec. size

There is no high-temperature setting on this body — long-video planning is power planning. Canon's specifications put one fresh LP-E6NH at roughly 1 hour 20 minutes of 4K 59.94 recording at room temperature, and a single movie can run up to 6 hours if the power lasts.
