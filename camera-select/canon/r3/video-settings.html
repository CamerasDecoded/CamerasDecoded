Video settings

How do you set up video on an EOS R3 — 6K RAW at 2600 megabits, 4K 120p slow motion, Canon Log 3 at ISO 800, and the card rules that decide what records where?

camerasdecoded.com/camera-select/canon/r3/video-settings
The answer — share this
6K RAW Light for the edit, 4K IPB for the everyday take, Canon Log 3 only when the footage will be graded — and the card matched to the codec, because RAW, 4K 120p and 4K 60p ALL-I will not record to an SD card at all.
Movie rec. size: 6K RAW Light / 4K IPB  ·  Card: CFexpress VPG400 for RAW, HFR, 4K 60p ALL-I  ·  Canon Log settings → Canon Log: On (C.LOG3)  ·  ISO: 800+ for full dynamic range  ·  View Assist: On  ·  Heat: Auto Power Off Temperature → High
Cameras Decoded — Camera Select
Step 1

Pick the codec, then the card

MENU→Shooting→Movie rec. size

Choose the codec for the job. 6K RAW (6000×3164) comes in Standard and Light: at 59.94p, Standard runs 2,600 Mbps — about 18.7 gigabytes a minute — and Light runs 1,800 Mbps; at 29.97p, Standard is 2,000 and Light is 900. 4K DCI and UHD offer ALL-I (each frame compressed alone, better for editing) and IPB (Standard and Light): 4K 59.94p ALL-I is 940 Mbps, IPB Standard is 230, IPB Light is 120. Now the card rule, and it is absolute — the manual states it directly: RAW movies, 4K 119.9/100p High Frame Rate movies, and 4K 59.94/50p ALL-I cannot be recorded to SD cards. Pressing the movie shooting button will not start recording. Those three go to the CFexpress card, rated VPG400, or they do not go at all. And before recording 4K, format with Low level format — a slow card is the most common reason a take fails.

Step 2

Log for graded work only

MENU→Shooting→Canon Log settings→Canon Log→On (C.Log3)

Canon Log 3 is for footage you will grade — and only then. Set Canon Log to On (C.Log3) and the camera records 10-bit YCbCr 4:2:2 with a color space of BT.709, BT.2020 or Cinema Gamut. The dynamic range is approximately 1600% at ISO 800 or higher — and that floor matters: manually setting ISO 100-640 narrows the dynamic range, so the default C-Log3 range starts at ISO 800. The image on the monitor will look dark and flat — that is the log working. Turn View Assist. On for a clearer display while you shoot, and know what it does and does not do: it does not affect the movies recorded to the card, and it is not used for HDMI output — the file stays flat Canon Log. Canon's own cautions: horizontal banding on dark, flat subjects, and noise on skies and white walls — record brighter and adjust in grading. For RAW movies, Canon recommends C.Log3 to protect highlight gradation.

Step 3

True slow motion, no sound

MENU→Shooting→High Frame Rate

High Frame Rate is true slow motion, not a trick. 4K at 119.88/100 fps (ALL-I, 1,880 Mbps — CFexpress only) and Full HD at 239.76/200 fps (ALL-I, 680 Mbps). The files are recorded as 29.97/25 fps movies, so 119.88p plays back at one-quarter speed and 239.76p at one-eighth. Three limits to know: sound is not recorded in High Frame Rate movies — and headphones will not help, no sound is audible; 239.8/200 fps is not available with SD/SDHC cards; and a High Frame Rate take stops automatically at 89 minutes 29 seconds. The screen may flicker under fluorescent light while recording HFR, and Movie digital IS may not keep up at 239.8/200 fps.

Step 4

Plan for heat, not just cards

MENU→Shooting→Auto Power Off Temperature→High

Heat is the real limit on long takes. Set Auto Power Off Temperature to High to reduce how often the camera turns itself off for high card temperature — and know the trade: the cards may become hot on High, so wait until the card has cooled down before removing it. Watch the icons: a white icon means the camera is getting warm; a red icon means movie recording will soon terminate automatically — and after an auto-stop, you cannot record movies or shoot stills until the camera has cooled down. 4K and High Frame Rate recording heat the camera faster than regular movies. On power: a full LP-E19 runs 6K RAW at 29.97p for about 3 hours 10 minutes at room temperature, 4K for about 3 hours 20. And one workflow trick: RAW+MP4 under Record func records a RAW movie to Card 1 and an MP4 to Card 2 with the same file number — the MP4 is your edit proxy.

Try it now

Record one 6K RAW Light clip and one 4K 120p clip — then play them back and check the file sizes on the card. Set Auto Power Off Temperature to High and turn View Assist. On, so the next log shoot is ready before it starts.
