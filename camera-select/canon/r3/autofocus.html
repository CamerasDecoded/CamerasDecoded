Autofocus, set up right

How do you set up autofocus on an EOS R3 — Servo AF with subject detection for people, animals and motorsports vehicles, the five AF Cases, and Eye Control AF that follows your gaze?

camerasdecoded.com/camera-select/canon/r3/autofocus
The answer — share this
Servo AF, Subject to detect named exactly — People, Animals, or motorsports Vehicles — a Flexible Zone for control or Whole area AF with tracking for movers, the Case matched to the motion, and Eye Control calibrated so the focus point follows your gaze.
AF operation: Servo AF  ·  Subject to detect: People / Animals / Vehicles  ·  AF area: Flexible Zone AF / Whole area AF  ·  Case: 1-4 or A  ·  Eye Control: Set-up → Eye control → Calibration (CAL)
Cameras Decoded — Camera Select
Step 1

Servo AF, and name the subject

MENU→AF→AF operation→Servo AF

Set AF operation to Servo AF for anything that moves — the camera keeps focusing as long as you hold the shutter halfway — and One-Shot AF for anything that does not. Then name the subject under Subject to detect: People (faces and heads first, then torso, then body), Animals (dogs, cats, birds — plus people), or Vehicles. Two details that catch owners out: Vehicles means two- or four-wheeled motorsports vehicles — Canon's own caution says detection may not be possible for ordinary passenger cars or bicycles — and eyes are not detected at all when Subject to detect is set to None. Keep Subject tracking On: with it Off, no tracking frame appears whatever the subject setting. The tracking frame sits gray until it overlaps an AF point, then turns white — white is the camera saying it has the subject.

Step 2

How much of the frame may it use?

MENU→AF→AF area

The AF area is the leash. Whole area AF lets the camera pick anywhere in the frame — the right choice for movers you cannot predict, and the one that pairs with Eye Control. Flexible Zone AF 1, 2 and 3 give you a zone you size and place — the control choice when you know where the action is. 1-point AF is a single point for a single spot, and Expand AF area surrounds it with helpers for a subject that will not sit still. Two moves worth learning: tap the screen to lock tracking on a subject across the whole frame (with Whole area AF), and press the AF point selection button to release the lock — the point returns to where it was. One trap: tapping the screen always focuses with One-Shot AF, even when the camera is set to Servo. And if the AF point turns orange, the camera could not focus — recompose and try again.

Step 3

Match the Case to the motion

MENU→AF→Case 1 - Case A

The five Cases are five combinations of tracking sensitivity and acceleration tracking — five answers to one question: how does your subject move? Case 1 is the versatile all-rounder for moving subjects in general. Case 2 keeps tracking through obstacles — a tennis player behind the net, a skier behind a gate. Case 3 snaps onto subjects that suddenly enter the AF points. Case 4 is for subjects that accelerate or decelerate quickly — motorsports, basketball. Case A adapts automatically to the subject's movement, and it is the honest default when you do not know what the day holds. Each Case is tunable: Tracking sensitivity (how stubbornly it holds the subject) and Accel./decel. tracking live beside the Cases on the same tab. Start with Case A; move to a numbered Case when you can name the motion.

Step 4

Look at it — it focuses there

MENU→Set-up→Eye control→Calibration (CAL)

Eye Control is the R3's signature move: infrared LEDs read the reflections off your cornea, and the focus point goes where you look. It needs calibration first: Set-up → Eye control → Calibration (CAL) → Start, then look at the indicator at five positions — center, right, left, top, bottom — without blinking until each disappears. Choose Refine and repeat it holding the camera vertically. Three rules that make it work: calibrate under the same eyewear conditions you shoot in (glasses or contacts — a different CAL number for each), stills only — Eye Control does not work in movie recording — and half-press to confirm: the pointer follows your gaze, the half-press locks the subject, and in Servo AF the camera keeps tracking it while you hold. With Whole area AF it focuses near the pointer; with any other area it moves the area to the pointer.

Step 5

Register the people you shoot

MENU→AF→Register people priority

If you shoot one person all day — a wedding, a portrait session, a season of one athlete — register their face and the camera puts them first for focus, brightness and color. Photograph the person under good light, facing you, no hat, mask or sunglasses, natural expression — or use a JPEG or HEIF already on the card. It works when Subject to detect is set to People. One responsibility rides with it: the camera stores their face image, so ask first, and delete the registered data before the camera changes hands.

Try it now

Set Servo AF, name your subject, pick Whole area AF and Case A — then calibrate Eye Control, all five positions plus Refine, and shoot a burst while looking from subject to subject. Turn on Eye detection and pick the near eye.
