First Setup

What should you change on a brand-new EOS R3 before your first shoot — a CFexpress Type B slot and an SD slot, RAW at 29 megabytes with a 150-frame burst, and a 30 fps electronic shutter?

Format both cards in the camera — Card 1 takes CFexpress Type B, Card 2 takes SD — write every still to both with Rec. to multiple, shoot RAW (or C-RAW at about half the file), pick the Electronic shutter for 30 fps, then register the whole setup to C1.

Step 1

Format both cards in the camera

MENU→Set-up→Format card

Formatting builds the folder structure the camera expects. This body formats one card at a time — pick Card 1, format, then Card 2. Card 1 takes CFexpress Type B, Card 2 takes SD, SDHC or SDXC (UHS-II or UHS-I). For movie work, Canon says to use a CFexpress card rated VPG400 — the fastest codecs demand it. Before recording 4K movies, tick Low level format on the format screen: it formats every recordable sector, which is what a slow or tired card needs. Low-level formatting takes longer, and you can cancel it partway — the normal format is already done by then. Formatting erases everything on the selected card, including protected images.

Step 2

Decide where the files land

MENU→Set-up→Record func+card/folder sel.

Out of the box the camera records to one card and the second slot sits idle. For stills, set Rec options to Rec. to multiple — every shot lands on both cards, and the number of shots shown is for the card with less free space. If you shoot stills and video, the separate setting does the split for you: movies to Card 1, stills to Card 2, automatically. Two limits worth knowing: Rec. to multiple is not available for RAW movies, 4K 59.94p ALL-I, or 4K High Frame Rate movies — those go to one card only — and with Rec. to multiple, Card full appears when one card fills and shooting stops, even if the other card has room.

Step 3

Shoot RAW, at full quality

MENU→Shooting→Image quality

Set Image quality to RAW — and know the trade this body offers. A full RAW file is 29.3 megabytes, and the electronic-shutter burst runs to 150 frames — five seconds at 30 fps — on either card. C-RAW is 15.1 megabytes, about half the file, and the burst jumps to 420 frames on a CFexpress card (320 on a UHS-I SD card). For a sports body that shoots bursts all day, that difference is real: C-RAW buys nearly three times the burst for half the storage. RAW and C-RAW are always generated in 3:2, whatever aspect ratio you set.

Step 4

Pick the shutter for the job

MENU→Shooting→Shutter mode

The R3 has three shutters, and the speed lives in the choice. Electronic: 30 frames a second at H+, blackout-free — but only when the shutter speed is 1/125 or faster and flicker reduction is off; aperture changes and zooming during the burst slow it down. Mechanical and Electronic 1st-curtain both top out at 12 fps. The electronic shutter also reaches 1/64000 (in Tv or M mode) for wide apertures in hard sun. Flash sync flips the ranking: Electronic 1/180, Mechanical 1/200, Electronic 1st-curtain 1/250 — the fastest sync is the middle shutter. Two honest cautions about the electronic shutter: fast-moving subjects can look distorted (rolling shutter), and flickering light or another camera's flash can band the frame. And the party trick: Custom high-speed continuous shooting fires up to 50 shots at 195 fps, with the exposure locked from the first frame.

Step 5

Save the setup to C1

MENU→Set-up→Custom shooting mode (C1-C3)→Register settings

Register everything you just built with Custom shooting mode (C1-C3): pick Register settings, choose C1, confirm — the current camera settings are saved to the mode dial, one twist away whenever you need them. Still and movie Custom modes are separate, so register each side on its own. Then confirm the firmware under Set-up → Firmware — current is 2.1.0 (May 2026). One trap from Canon's own update notes: a firmware update deletes the registered data stored in the camera — save your settings to the card first with Save/load cam settings on card, then update.

Try it now

Do the steps now, in order, then save the setup with Custom shooting mode — one set to C1 — and recall it from the mode dial to prove it returns. Finish by checking the firmware — current is 2.1.0 — and setting your copyright information.
