Common fixes

What fixes the Fujifilm X100VI’s known surprises — dark frames, a missing teleconverter crop, vanished detection, off-center close-ups?

camerasdecoded.com/camera-select/fujifilm/x100vi/common-fixes
The answer — share this
Dark frames in daylight: the ND FILTER is still on — a persistent 4 EV menu state with a selector-hold shortcut. A teleconverter crop missing from your RAW: RAW is never magnified — only JPEG/HEIF keep the crop. Detection that vanished: face/eye and subject detection switch each other off. Off-center close-ups in the OVF: parallax — the corrected frame tells the truth at about 50 cm. And with flash in S or M modes, the manual’s ceiling is a shutter speed slower than 1/2000 s.
ND: SHOOTING SETTING → ND FILTER  ·  Teleconverter: SHOOTING SETTING → DIGITAL TELE-CONV. (JPEG/HEIF only)  ·  Detection: AF/MF SETTING → FACE/EYE DETECTION SETTING / SUBJECT DETECTION SETTING (one at a time)  ·  Finder: AF/MF SETTING → CORRECTED AF FRAME → ON  ·  Flash: FLASH SETTING → FLASH FUNCTION SETTING (slower than 1/2000 s in S/M)
Cameras Decoded — Camera Select
Fix 1

The forgotten ND

MENU→SHOOTING SETTING→ND FILTER

The ND FILTER is a persistent menu state worth 4 EV. Left on from yesterday, it makes every frame four stops dark while the camera compensates with slow shutter speeds and high ISO. Check its state first whenever exposures stop making sense; rotate-and-hold the viewfinder selector toggles it without a menu.

Fix 2

The crop that isn’t in the RAW

MENU→SHOOTING SETTING→DIGITAL TELE-CONV.

DIGITAL TELE-CONV. offers 50 mm (1.4×) and 70 mm (2×) — and auto-crops sizes as it goes: 50 mm takes L pictures to M; 70 mm takes L and M to S. But RAW pictures cannot be magnified: the RAW file always keeps the full 35 mm frame, and only JPEG/HEIF record the crop. Magnified copies of a RAW are made afterwards in playback: RAW CONVERSION → DIGITAL TELE-CONV.

Fix 3

Detection that vanished

MENU→AF/MF SETTING→FACE/EYE DETECTION SETTING

Face/eye detection and subject detection switch each other off, in both directions, in the manual’s own words — enabling one automatically selects OFF for the other. The “vanished” system was traded, not broken. After any detection change, check the other system’s state. Face detection’s own limits, per the troubleshooting chapter: faces obscured by sunglasses, hats or hair, and faces too small in the frame.

Fix 4

Parallax, not a focus fault

MENU→AF/MF SETTING→CORRECTED AF FRAME

The OVF’s window sits a slight distance from the lens, so at close range the finder’s framing and focus position differ from the recorded picture — parallax. The bright frame shifts at half-press to show the actual crop; CORRECTED AF FRAME adds a guide frame for the true focus position at about 50 cm, and shows nothing closer than 50 cm. The EVF sees through the lens at 100% coverage; the ERF window is the middle path, with SINGLE POINT AF only.

Fix 5

The wide-open ceiling, and the flash rule

MENU→SHOOTING SETTING→SHUTTER TYPE

The leaf shutter’s mechanical maximum varies with aperture, in the manual’s words: from 1/2000 s at f/2 to 1/4000 s at f/4.5 — wide open, 1/2000 is the ceiling. Flash adds its own rule: in S or M exposure modes, choose shutter speeds slower than 1/2000 s when using the flash (choose MECHANICAL or MECHANICAL + ELECTRONIC — the flash cannot be used with the electronic shutter). The built-in flash’s effective range is about 0.3–7.4 m at ISO 1600; there is no sync terminal, so external flash works from the hot shoe or by optical command.
