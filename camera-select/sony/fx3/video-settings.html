Sony · FX3 · Pro lesson

Video, set up right

What is the right video setup on the Sony FX3 — six codecs including XAVC S-I DCI 4K, native 4K 120p, Log Shooting with Cine EI at Base ISO 800 and 12800, shutter angle, and the XLR handle?

camerasdecoded.com/camera-select/sony/fx3/video-settings
The answer — share this
Choose the codec for the job — XAVC S-I for editing, XAVC HS for small files — shoot log through Log Shooting Setting (Flexible ISO for run-and-gun, Cine EI at Base ISO 800 or 12800 for controlled shoots), set the shutter by angle (180° is the film look), and feed the XLR handle for real audio.
Codec: XAVC S-I 4K 600M 4:2:2 10-bit  ·  Log: Flexible ISO / Cine EI Quick / Cine EI  ·  Base ISO: 800 / 12800  ·  Shutter: Angle, 180°  ·  4K 120p: native, no license  ·  Audio: XLR handle, 4ch
Cameras Decoded — Camera Select
Step 1

Pick the codec for the job

MENU→Shooting→Image Quality/Rec→File Format

Six formats, one decision. XAVC S-I 4K (Intra) compresses every frame on its own — the most suitable for editing — at up to 600 Mbps 4:2:2 10-bit at 59.94p. XAVC HS 4K (H.265) holds the same quality in smaller files, but needs HEVC-capable playback. XAVC S 4K (H.264) is the compatible pick. XAVC S-I DCI 4K (4096×2160, firmware 3.00+) is the cinema-width frame. And 4K 120p is native — no license, no unlock, unlike the ZV-E1. The card must match the codec: XAVC S-I 4K needs CFexpress Type A VPG200+ or SDXC V90; slow-motion S-I 4K at 1200 Mbps needs CFexpress Type A VPG200+ only. SteadyShot Active narrows the angle of view and is unavailable at 119.88p/100p and above — and on a tripod, turn SteadyShot off entirely.

Step 2

Log Shooting, three ways

MENU→Shooting→Image Quality/Rec→Log Shooting Setting

Log Shooting Setting offers three modes. Flexible ISO records S-Log material while you adjust ISO to the scene — the run-and-gun pick. Cine EI shoots at one of two Base ISOs — 800 (L) or 12800 (H) — for maximum dynamic range and suppressed noise; you adjust brightness with the Exposure Index, ±2 EV from the Base ISO (200–3200 EI at Base 800; 3200–51200 EI at Base 12800). Cine EI Quick is Cine EI with the Base ISO switching automatically as you adjust EI. The screen shows it as 800EI/6.0E/L — EI value, latitude, Base ISO — where the second number is the steps of brightness assigned above 18% gray. Two fine-print items: Gamma Display Assist is locked to Off while Log Shooting is on — use Display LUT instead (Setup → Display Option), with Select LUT offering S-Log3, s709, 709(800%) and sixteen user LUTs. And Embed LUT File is locked Off on SD/SDHC cards — CFexpress only.

Step 3

Exposure, the cinema way

MENU→Exposure/Color→Exposure→Shutter Mode

Two settings make the exposure system feel like a cinema camera. Shutter Mode (firmware 6.00+) switches the shutter from speed to angle — 180° is the film look (at 24fps, that is 1/48s), and the angle stays constant when the frame rate changes, which is the whole point. Angle is active in Shutter Priority and Manual, or in Flexible Exposure with the shutter set to Manual. Note: the Speed and Angle settings are stored separately — switching between them does not carry the value over. Exposure Ctrl Type picks the exposure method: P/A/S/M Mode works like stills, or Flexible Exp. Mode, where aperture, shutter speed and ISO each flip between Auto and Manual on their own — and the Custom Buttons lesson already put those three toggles on C1, C3 and Down. Zebra is the exposure check: set the level (70–100+) and register correct-exposure and overexposure checks to Custom 1 and 2.

Step 4

S&Q, proxies, XLR audio

MENU→Shooting→Image Quality/Rec→FPS

S&Q runs through the FPS menu (firmware 7.00+): set Mode Settings to Fixed/Variable, pick Variable, and the shooting frame rate runs to 240fps — ten times slow at 23.98p. Variable mode records no audio, by design. Proxy Settings records a small file alongside the main take — XAVC HS HD at 1080p or XAVC S HD at 720p — for phone transfers and quick uploads; proxies are not playable on the camera, and proxy recording is impossible at 119.88p/100p. For sound, the supplied XLR handle takes two XLR inputs (LINE/MIC/MIC+48V) plus a third jack, and records four channels — the Common Fixes lesson covers the switch that catches everyone out. And the long-take pair: Fan Control (Auto / Minimum / Off in Rec / Always Off) and Auto Power OFF Temp. on High — HD about 120 minutes, 4K about 90 minutes continuous at 25 °C, on a tripod.

Try it now

Set XAVC S-I 4K at 59.94p 600M 4:2:2 10-bit, Log Shooting to Flexible ISO with Display LUT on, and the shutter to Angle at 180° — then roll a ten-second take. Finish by shooting a 120fps slow-motion clip and recording a few seconds through the XLR handle.
