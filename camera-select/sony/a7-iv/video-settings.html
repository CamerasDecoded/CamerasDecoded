Video settings, in depth

What is the right video setup on the a7 IV — format, profile, and the settings around them?

camerasdecoded.com/camera-select/sony/a7-iv/video-settings
Step 1

Pick the format by the edit, not the spec sheet

MENU→Shooting→Image Quality→File Format

XAVC HS 4K records HEVC — smallest files, hardest on your editing machine. XAVC S 4K is the balanced default. XAVC S-I records every frame individually (Intra): the easiest footage to edit and by far the biggest files. Choose by what happens after the shoot.

The three 4K formats
XAVC HS 4KSmallest files. HEVC — older computers struggle to play it smoothly.
XAVC S 4KThe default. Balanced size and editing load for most work.
XAVC S-I 4KEvery frame stored whole. Easiest to edit, huge files, fast cards required.

Step 2

There is no Log menu on this camera — use Picture Profile

MENU→Exposure/Color→Color/Tone→Picture Profile

Newer Sony bodies have a Log Shooting menu. The a7 IV does not. Log here is a Picture Profile: PP11 is S-Cinetone, finished-looking straight out of camera. PP8 is S-Log3 with S-Gamut3.Cine — the grading profile. PP9 is S-Log3 with the wider S-Gamut3.

Know this: S-Log3 shows noise more readily than standard profiles. Sony’s own guidance: expose brighter than feels natural — the dynamic range cost is real, and the noise you avoid is worth more.
Step 3

Zebra on, before you trust the screen

MENU→Exposure/Color→Zebra Display→Zebra Display + Zebra Level

The rear screen lies in bright light; zebra stripes do not. Turn Zebra Display on and leave Zebra Level at its default 70 for a general exposure read, or use a custom level as an overexposure alarm. Note: zebra does not appear on devices connected over HDMI.

Step 4

Movie stabilization: Active, with two exceptions

MENU→Shooting→Image Stabilization→SteadyShot (movie)

Active gives the strongest correction for handheld work. Two exceptions: at 200mm and longer Sony recommends Standard, and on a tripod turn stabilization off entirely. Active is also unavailable at 120p/100p, and it narrows the angle of view slightly.

Step 5

Focus speed is a creative setting

MENU→Focus→AF/MF→AF Transition Speed · AF Subj. Shift Sensitivity

AF Transition Speed runs 1 (slow) to 7 (fast), default 5. Slow racks (2–3) read as deliberate and cinematic; the default reads as documentary. AF Subj. Shift Sensitivity decides how eagerly focus jumps to a new subject — 1 keeps it locked on yours.

Step 6

Shooting long? Set the temperature limit honestly

MENU→Setup→Power Setting Option→Auto Power OFF Temp.

High lets the camera record longer before the heat shutdown. It is a real tool for long event records — with Sony’s real warnings attached.

Sony’s warning, plainly: on High, do not hold the camera while shooting — use a tripod. Holding it for a long period may cause low-temperature burns, and recordable time may still not improve in some conditions.
Try it now

Shoot the same 20-second clip three ways — PP11, then PP8 exposed normally, then PP8 exposed a stop brighter. Compare the shadows. You will never guess at Log exposure again.
