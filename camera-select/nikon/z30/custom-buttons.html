Custom button map

How do you map the Nikon Z30's controls — two Fn buttons with useful defaults, back-button focus that takes two settings, the OK button's tracking rule, and a video side that has to be set separately?

camerasdecoded.com/camera-select/nikon/z30/custom-buttons
The answer — share this
Keep Fn2 on focus and area mode — its default is the best shortcut on the body — put Subject tracking on Fn1, wire back-button focus with AF-ON on AE-L/AF-L plus a6 set to AF-ON only, and remember the OK button always starts tracking in auto-area modes no matter what you assigned it to.
Fn1: Subject tracking (default: White balance)  ·  Fn2: KEEP Focus mode/AF-area mode (default)  ·  AE-L/AF-L: AF-ON + Custom Setting a6 → AF-ON only  ·  OK: Select center focus point (auto-area modes override it to tracking)  ·  Lens control ring: Aperture (photo) / Power aperture (video)  ·  Video record button: + RAW or Silent photography  ·  Video side: Custom Setting g2 — set it separately, f2 does not carry over

Step 1

Two Fn buttons — keep one default

MENU→Custom Settings Menu→f2 Custom controls (shooting)

By default, Fn1 is white balance (hold it and rotate the main command dial to choose, the sub-command dial for the preset) and Fn2 is Focus mode/AF-area mode (hold it and rotate the main dial for the focus mode, the sub dial for the area mode). The map: keep Fn2 exactly as it is — it is the fastest focus control on the body. Give Fn1 to Subject tracking — pressing it in an auto-area mode turns the focus point into the targeting reticle and starts tracking. White balance moves to the i menu, one tap away. With a power zoom lens, firmware 1.10 adds Power zoom + on Fn1 and Power zoom − on Fn2 — assigning one auto-assigns the other — but in self-portrait mode the Fn1/Fn2 power zoom is disabled.

Step 2

Back-button focus takes two settings

MENU→Custom Settings Menu→f2 Custom controls (shooting)
MENU→Custom Settings Menu→a6 AF activation

Assign AF-ON to the AE-L/AF-L button in Custom Setting f2 — then set Custom Setting a6 (AF activation) to AF-ON only. Without the second setting, the shutter-release button keeps focusing too, and you have two focus buttons fighting each other. With a6 set, the shutter only fires; the thumb focuses. The a6 screen also carries an Out-of-focus release sub-option — Enable lets the shutter fire any time, Disable keeps the focus lock.

Step 3

The OK rule and the spare buttons

MENU→Custom Settings Menu→f2 Custom controls (shooting)

With Auto-area AF, Auto-area AF (people) or Auto-area AF (animals) selected, the OK button functions only to initiate subject-tracking AF, regardless of its f2 assignment. So put it on Select center focus point — in every other area mode it snaps the point back to the center in one press, and in auto-area modes it tracks anyway. The video record button is the spare in photo mode: + RAW adds a RAW file to the next JPEG-only shot, Silent photography toggles the electronic shutter, MY MENU opens your menu, and Access top item in MY MENU jumps straight to whatever you put first. The lens control ring takes Focus (M/A), Aperture, Exposure compensation, or ISO sensitivity — the ISO role is the quiet gem for manual shooters. In manual focus mode the ring only adjusts focus, whatever you assigned.

Step 4

The video side is a second map

MENU→Custom Settings Menu→g2 Custom controls

Custom Setting g2 is the video side, and f2 does not carry over. In video, Fn1 and Fn2 become a pair: Power aperture (open) on Fn1 and Power aperture (close) on Fn2 gives you button-controlled aperture while recording, or Exposure compensation + on Fn1 and − on Fn2 for button-controlled brightness. The shutter-release button can take a still photo while you record (Take photos), and the lens control ring takes Power aperture in video. The i menu is separate too — f1 for photos, g1 for video — so put Microphone sensitivity and Electronic VR on the video i menu and leave the photo one alone.

Try it now

Set the full map in f2: Fn1 on Subject tracking, AE-L/AF-L on AF-ON with a6 set to AF-ON only, OK on Select center focus point, the ring on Aperture. Test that the shutter no longer focuses. Then set g2 for video.
