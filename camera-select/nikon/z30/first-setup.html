First Setup

What should you change on a brand-new Nikon Z30 before your first shoot — one SD slot that takes UHS-I only, RAW at 14 bits with a 36-frame buffer, no viewfinder at all, and a U1 slot to save the whole setup to?

Step 1

Format the card in the camera

MENU→Setup Menu→Format memory card

Formatting builds the folder structure the camera expects and clears a card that has lived in other devices. This body has one memory card slot, reached through the shared battery-chamber cover, and it takes SD, SDHC and SDXC cards — UHS-I only. The faster UHS-II cards still work, but they run at UHS-I speed, so do not pay the premium for this body. While you are in the Setup Menu, set the clock too — Time zone and date sits in the same tab. And build the house rule now: one card, one copy — with no second slot and no backup recording, the copy you make at the computer after every shoot that matters is the only backup there is. Formatting permanently deletes everything on the card, so empty it first.

Step 2

NEF + JPEG fine, at 14 bits

MENU→Photo Shooting Menu→Image quality
MENU→Photo Shooting Menu→RAW recording

Set Image quality to NEF (RAW) + JPEG fine — the RAW for the edit, the JPEG for sharing straight from the camera. Then choose the depth: RAW recording offers 12-bit or 14-bit. 14-bit holds more color data and costs more: a large RAW file is about 26.8 megabytes and the buffer runs to 36 frames. 12-bit is about 21.4 megabytes with a 44-frame buffer. For most shooting, 14-bit is the right default — the extra color data is what saves a sky or a skin tone in the edit. Switch to 12-bit only when you are shooting long bursts and the buffer matters more than the last bit of color. JPEG fine on its own is about 10.4 megabytes a file, with an 82-frame buffer.

Step 3

Lens VR — because the body has none

MENU→Photo Shooting Menu→Vibration reduction

This body has no in-body stabilization. The Vibration reduction item in the Photo Shooting Menu drives the lens — and the manual says it plainly: the options available vary with the lens, and vibration reduction may be unavailable with some lenses. Set it to Normal for everyday shooting; Sport steadies the view for a moving subject and for panning. The honest consequence: with a lens that has no VR of its own, there is no stabilization at all — your shutter speed and your stance are doing the work. The kit 16-50mm has VR; the 28mm f/2.8 and 40mm f/2 primes do not.

Step 4

Save the setup to U1

MENU→Setup Menu→Save user settings

This body has a mode dial with U1, U2 and U3 — three slots that hold a full setup each. Set the camera up the way you want it, then go to Setup Menu → Save user settings → Save to U1 → Save settings. What gets saved: the photo and video menu options, the Custom Settings, the shooting mode, shutter speed (S/M), aperture (A/M), flexible program (P), exposure compensation and flash compensation. After that, turning the dial to U1 recalls the whole setup in one move — keep U2 for video and U3 for the next thing you shoot often.

Step 5

Know the body you bought

MENU→Setup Menu→Firmware version

A 20.9-megapixel DX CMOS sensor with the EXPEED 6 engine, 209 focus points, and a 3-inch vari-angle touch screen that flips forward for self-portraits — there is no viewfinder, the monitor is the finder. No built-in flash — the hot shoe takes an optional flash unit. Check the firmware while you are here: Setup Menu → Firmware version should read C 1.20 (May 2025). The 1.20 update fixed a rare freeze when the camera was turned on with the monitor opened from its stored position, and 1.10 added support for the longer-lasting EN-EL25a battery (about 360 shots, vs about 330 for the EN-EL25). Battery life is short either way — carry a spare, and charge over USB-C when you can.

Try it now

Format the card, set the clock, NEF plus JPEG fine at 14 bits, lens VR on Normal — then save it all to U1 and check the firmware reads C 1.20. Flip the monitor forward to feel self-portrait mode lock the buttons. Copy the card when you get home: that copy is your backup now.
