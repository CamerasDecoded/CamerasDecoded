Video settings, in depth

How do you set up video on a Nikon Z30 — 4K 30p with no crop at 144 Mbps, takes up to 125 minutes, slow motion with no sound, a front REC lamp that talks in flashes, and no headphone jack to check any of it?

camerasdecoded.com/camera-select/nikon/z30/video-settings
The answer — share this
Shoot 4K 30p with no crop at 144 Mbps and quality fixed at High, plan on about 35 minutes per 4K take because heat is the limit (125 minutes is the Full HD figure), use Electronic VR knowing it crops the angle of view, and check your sound with a test clip — there is no headphone jack on this body.
4K UHD 30p/25p/24p: 144 Mbps, quality fixed High, NO crop  ·  Take length: up to 125 min (Full HD) — about 35 min at 4K (heat)  ·  1080 60p: 56 High / 28 Normal  ·  Slow-mo: 1080 30p ×4, 36 Mbps, 3-min max, NO sound  ·  Electronic VR: crops the angle of view; off at 1080 120p + slow-mo  ·  Mic: Auto / Manual 1–20 / Off — red means too high  ·  NO headphone jack  ·  REC lamp: slow flash = card short, 2× fast = battery low, 4× fast = hot  ·  NO log — Flat Picture Control is the flat option

Step 1

4K, no crop — and the heat limit

MENU→Video Recording Menu→Frame size/frame rate
MENU→Video Recording Menu→Video quality

4K UHD (3840×2160) at 30p, 25p or 24p is full width with no additional crop — the whole DX sensor, so a 16mm lens stays a 16mm lens — at 144 Mbps with quality fixed at High; there is no Normal quality at 4K. At 1080p you get 120p and 100p (quality fixed at High), 60p and 50p at 56 Mbps High / 28 Normal, and 30p/25p/24p at 28 High / 14 Normal. Each shot can run up to 125 minutes — but that figure is for Full HD at 24p/25p. At 4K, heat limits a take to about 35 minutes in practice, and recording may end early if the battery discharges or the camera temperature rises. A 32 GB card holds about 28 minutes of 4K 30p, about 72 minutes of 1080 60p at High. Cards of 32 GB or less may split a long take into up to 8 files of up to 4 GB each.

Step 2

Two stabilizers — and the crop

MENU→Video Recording Menu→Vibration reduction
MENU→Video Recording Menu→Electronic VR

Lens VR steadies the optics — set Vibration reduction to Normal in the video menu. Electronic VR is digital stabilization: it works, but it reduces the angle of view, slightly increasing the apparent focal length — the 16mm end of the kit zoom gets tighter at arm's length, which is exactly where a vlogger feels it. Use Electronic VR when handholding and walking; turn it off when framing matters or the camera is on a tripod. It is disabled at 1080 120p/100p and in slow-motion, so those modes lean on lens VR alone. Autofocus is full-time AF-F with eye detection for people and pets (g3 AF speed and g4 AF tracking sensitivity tune how fast and how sticky it is).

Step 3

Real slow motion — with rules

MENU→Video Recording Menu→Frame size/frame rate

1080 30p ×4 reads at 120p and plays at 30p (10 s recorded becomes about 40 s of playback), 25p ×4 reads at 100p, 24p ×5 reads at 120p. Bitrate is 36 Mbps at 30p ×4, 29 Mbps at 24p ×5. The rules: sound is not recorded, the take caps at 3 minutes, and four things switch off — face/eye detection in the people and animals area modes, flicker reduction, Electronic VR, and HDMI output while recording (plus timecode recording). So plan the shot: focus manually or lock focus before the action, light it well, and do not expect the camera to find a face mid-take. For smooth motion with sound and autofocus, 1080 120p is the better tool.

Step 4

The mic, the lamp, and no jack

MENU→Video Recording Menu→Microphone sensitivity
MENU→Setup Menu→REC lamp brightness

Microphone sensitivity offers Auto, Manual 1–20, or Off — red on the level meter means too high, so turn it down. The Attenuator reduces gain in loud environments; Wind noise reduction is a low-cut filter for the built-in mic only. The 3.5 mm mic jack takes an external mic with plug-in power. There is no headphone jack, so you cannot monitor audio live — the test clip is the monitor. Record ten seconds, play it back, check the level and the wind, then shoot. The REC lamp on the front talks in flashes: slow when the remaining card time is short, twice quickly (repeating) when the battery is low in video mode, 4 times quickly (repeating) when the camera temperature rises. Its brightness lives at Setup Menu → REC lamp brightness — set [Video recording] to [Off] to keep the lamp out of your videos. Custom Setting g7 (Red REC frame indicator) puts a red border around the display while recording. File type: MOV records Linear PCM audio, MP4 records AAC. And there is no log profile — no N-Log, no HLG; the Flat Picture Control is the flat option, and the 20 Creative Picture Controls are the in-camera looks.

Step 5

Self-portrait mode is the vlogging position

MENU→Setup Menu→Self-portrait mode

Flip the monitor forward and the camera switches to self-portrait mode: the display mirrors (the saved image is from the camera's perspective), touch controls set the self-timer and exposure compensation in photo mode and exposure compensation in video mode, and every control except the power switch, shutter-release button, video-record button, photo/video selector and mode dial is disabled. Release mode is fixed to Self-timer, video focus mode is fixed to Full-time AF, and Long exposure NR, Auto bracketing, HDR and Exposure delay mode cannot be used. If you want the monitor forward without the lock, disable it at Setup Menu → Self-portrait mode → Disable. Video ISO runs 100–25600, with Maximum sensitivity capping auto ISO — set the cap before the shoot, not during it.

Try it now

Flip the monitor forward and feel self-portrait mode lock the buttons. Set 4K 30p, Electronic VR on, mic on Manual 10, g7 Red REC frame on. Record a ten-second test clip and play it back — that test clip is your headphone jack.
