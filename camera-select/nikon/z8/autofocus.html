Autofocus, set up right

How do you set up autofocus and subject detection on a Nikon Z8 so it holds your subject?

camerasdecoded.com/camera-select/nikon/z8/autofocus
The answer — share this
Run AF-C with Wide-area AF (C1) sized to your subject and Subject detection set to People — or Auto when the subject keeps changing — and let 3D-tracking carry anything that crosses the frame.
Focus mode: AF-C  ·  AF-area mode: Wide-area AF (C1) or Auto-area AF  ·  Subject detection: People or Auto  ·  Set at: Photo Shooting Menu → AF/MF subject detection options
Cameras Decoded — Camera Select
Step 1

AF-C for movers, AF-S for still life

MENU→Photo Shooting Menu→Focus mode

AF-S locks focus at the half-press, and by default the camera will not fire until focus is achieved (focus priority). AF-C tracks continuously and, by default, fires the moment you press (release priority). There is no AF-A on the Z8 — Nikon makes you choose, and anything that moves gets AF-C. AF-F exists too, but only in video mode, where it focuses full-time.

Step 2

Name the subject

MENU→Photo Shooting Menu→AF/MF subject detection options

Subject detection offers Auto, People, Animal, Birds, Vehicle, Airplanes, and Subject detection off. Vehicle is a single category on this body — cars, motorcycles, trains, airplanes and bicycles are all detected under it. Animal covers dogs, cats and birds, faces first and then eyes; Birds is also a dedicated category with its own behaviour for feathered subjects. Auto gives priority to people, then animals, then vehicles and airplanes. Remember: the Video Recording Menu keeps a separate detection choice of its own.

Know this: Nikon's cautions are honest — detection may miss faces that are too large or small in the frame, too brightly or dimly lit, wearing sunglasses, or with eyes hidden by hair or fur. When it hunts, get closer or give it a cleaner subject.
Step 3

Pick — and size — the area it hunts in

MENU→Photo Shooting Menu→AF-area mode

Wide-area AF (S) and (L) are the everyday areas. Wide-area AF (C1) and (C2) are the flagship's own: custom areas whose size you set by holding the focus-mode button and pressing the arrow keys — match the zone to the subject. 3D-tracking follows a subject you place the point on, across the whole frame; Auto-area AF searches everything. Dynamic-area AF (S/M/L) backs up a chosen point with its neighbours for predictable movers, and Pinpoint AF is the still-life specialist — a point smaller than Single-point, AF-S only.

Step 4

Tune blocked shot response

MENU→Custom Settings Menu→a3 Focus tracking with lock-on

Blocked shot AF response sets how the camera reacts when something passes between you and your subject in AF-C. Toward 5 (Delayed), it holds on to your original subject through the interruption; toward 1 (Quick), it happily shifts to the crossing object. Note the fine print: with 3D-tracking selected, the behaviour is fixed at 3 regardless of this setting.

Step 5

Speed, and the frames before the moment

MENU→Custom Settings Menu→d3 Pre-Release Capture options

Continuous high-speed stills run at 20 fps; high-speed frame capture + adds C15, C30, C60 and C120 (JPEG) — feed it CFexpress or XQD cards rated at least 250 MB/s, as Nikon recommends. Pre-Release Capture buffers frames while the shutter is half-pressed and, on the full press, saves frames from just before it; the pre-release burst length is set at d3, and standby lasts up to 300 seconds.

Try it now

Set AF-C, People, Wide-area AF (C1) sized to a person — then 3D-track them crossing the room and feel the point travel. That is the setup working.
