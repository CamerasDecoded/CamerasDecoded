Autofocus, set up right

How do you set up autofocus on a Nikon Z fc — 209 focus points, AF-C for motion, eye detection for people and animals (dogs and cats only — no birds, no vehicles), and subject tracking on the OK button?

camerasdecoded.com/camera-select/nikon/zfc/autofocus
The answer — share this
AF-C for anything that moves, AF-S for what holds still; pick the area to match the shot — single-point for precision, wide-area for people, auto-area for tracking — and know the eye detection covers people and dogs and cats only, never birds or vehicles.
Focus mode: AF-C for motion, AF-S for stills  ·  AF-area: Single-point / Wide-area AF (L-people) / Auto-area AF (people)  ·  Eye detection: people + dogs/cats only  ·  Tracking: OK button in auto-area modes  ·  Low light: Custom Setting a10 Low-light AF (−4.5 EV)
Cameras Decoded — Camera Select
Step 1

AF-S for still, AF-C for moving

MENU→Photo Shooting Menu→Focus mode

AF-S (single-servo) focuses once and locks — for landscapes, portraits, anything that holds still. AF-C (continuous-servo) keeps focusing while you hold the shutter halfway, with predictive tracking that follows a subject moving toward or away from you — for people walking, kids, pets, anything in motion. AF-A (auto-servo, photos only) starts in AF-S and switches to AF-C if the subject starts moving; convenient, but it can hesitate at the switch, so choose deliberately when the shot matters. AF-F (full-time-servo, video only) keeps focusing continuously while you record. Focus down to -4.5 EV in low light with Custom Setting a10 Low-light AF on (without it, the floor is -3 EV) — and the AF-assist light reaches only about 1 to 3 meters, and a lens hood can block it.

Step 2

Pick the area to match the shot

MENU→Photo Shooting Menu→AF-area mode

Pinpoint AF (photos only) is the smallest point, for macro and precise work. Single-point AF is one point you place — the default for deliberate shooting. Dynamic-area AF (photos only) is a single point with helpers around it: if the subject slips off the point, the neighbors catch it. Wide-area AF (S) and (L) are zones you place and the camera focuses within. Wide-area AF (L-people) and (L-animals) are zones that find faces and eyes inside them. Auto-area AF lets the camera choose anywhere in the frame — with (people) and (animals) versions that prioritize faces and eyes. Ten modes is a lot to cycle through: Custom Setting a7 Limit AF-area mode selection lets you hide the ones you never use, so the i menu shows only your shortlist. And a4 Focus points used → Alternating points cuts the point count to a quarter when you want to move fast.

Step 3

Eyes — people and pets, nothing else

MENU→Photo Shooting Menu→AF-area mode→Wide-area AF (L-people)

With a people or animals area mode selected, the camera finds faces and eyes: a yellow border on the face, a yellow point on the eye. In AF-C the border stays yellow; in AF-S it turns green when focus locks. With more than one face or eye in the frame, the 4 and 2 buttons move between them. Eye detection works for video and stills. Now the limit, stated plainly: this body detects people and animals — dogs and cats. There is no bird detection and no vehicle detection; a bird or a car gets treated as an ordinary subject, and the wide-area zone is your tool for those. Animal detection can miss when the face is very large or very small in the frame, in poor light, when fur hides the eyes, when the eyes are close in color to the face, or when the animal moves fast — and the AF-assist light can bother some animals, so Custom Setting a11 turns it off.

Step 4

Point at it — the camera follows

Subject tracking lives in the auto-area modes. Press OK to bring up the tracking reticle, position it over your subject, press OK again — and the camera follows that subject around the frame. Press OK once more to end tracking, or the W (Q) button to leave tracking mode. One rule to know: in photo mode, if tracking was started in AF-C or AF-A by half-pressing the shutter, tracking lasts only while the shutter is held halfway — release, and it stops. Tracking can lose a subject that looks like its background, changes size, color or brightness, moves too fast, or leaves the frame.

Step 5

Touch is a focus tool too

Touch shutter/touch AF focuses and fires when you lift your finger (photos only), Touch AF focuses without firing — and tracks the subject in auto-area modes — and Position focus point just moves the point. Touch cannot focus in manual focus, cannot shoot during video recording, and takes one picture at a time even in continuous release mode.

Try it now

Set AF-C with Wide-area AF (L-people), point at a face, and watch the yellow border find the eye. Then try tracking: Auto-area AF (people), OK twice on one person, and walk them across the frame. Finish by opening Custom Setting a7 and hiding the area modes you will never use.
