Custom button map

How do you map the Z fc's controls — one Fn button, AE-L/AF-L for back-button focus, the OK button's tracking rule, and the lens control ring?

camerasdecoded.com/camera-select/nikon/zfc/custom-buttons
The answer — share this
One Fn button, AE-L/AF-L for back-button focus with a6 set to AF-ON only, the OK button for subject tracking in auto-area modes whatever else it is assigned, and the lens control ring for aperture or exposure compensation — the four mid-shoot changes, under your fingers.
Assign: Custom Settings → f2 Custom controls (shooting)  ·  Fn: Subject tracking  ·  AE-L/AF-L: AF-ON + a6 AF activation → AF-ON only  ·  OK: Select center focus point (tracking in auto-area modes)  ·  Lens ring: Aperture or Exposure compensation  ·  i menu: f1 (photo) / g1 (video)
Cameras Decoded — Camera Select
Step 1

Custom controls — f2 for stills, g2 for video

MENU→Custom Settings Menu→f2→Custom controls (shooting)

Custom Setting f2 Custom controls (shooting) assigns the buttons for stills; g2 Custom controls assigns them for video — set both sides, or the video side will surprise you the first time you roll. The assignable controls are the Fn button, the AE-L/AF-L button, the OK button, the video record button, the lens Fn and Fn2 buttons (on lenses that have them), and the lens control ring. One naming trap: the guide mentions Fn1 and Fn2 — those are buttons on the ML-L7 remote control, not on the camera. The body itself has one Fn button, on the front. Beside the desks, f1 Customize i menu (photos) and g1 Customize i menu (video) let you build a dashboard of the settings you actually touch.

Step 2

Separate focus from fire

MENU→Custom Settings Menu→f2→Custom controls (shooting)→AE-L/AF-L button
MENU→Custom Settings Menu→a6→AF activation

Back-button focus takes two settings, not one. First, assign AF-ON to the AE-L/AF-L button in f2. Second — the step people miss — set Custom Setting a6 AF activation to [AF-ON only]: now the camera will not focus when the shutter-release button is pressed halfway, and focus comes only from the button you assigned. The payoff: focus and fire are separate. Hold the button and AF-C tracks; release it and focus locks where it is; the shutter fires whenever you press it, focused or not. (a6 has a sub-option, Out-of-focus release: Enable lets the shutter fire at any time, Disable keeps the camera from firing out of focus.) If you prefer the shutter to keep focusing, leave a6 alone and give AE-L/AF-L AE lock (Hold) instead — exposure locks until you press the button again or the standby timer expires.

Step 3

The OK rule, the Fn button, the ring

MENU→Custom Settings Menu→f2→Custom controls (shooting)→Fn button

The OK button has a footnote in the manual that matters: with Auto-area AF, Auto-area AF (people) or Auto-area AF (animals) selected, the OK button functions only to initiate subject-tracking AF, regardless of its f2 assignment. So give it Select center focus point — in single-point and wide-area modes it snaps the point home, and in auto-area modes it tracks anyway. The Fn button gets Subject tracking: one press starts tracking from any area mode. The lens control ring takes Aperture or Exposure compensation (or Focus in manual focus) — and in MF mode the ring only adjusts focus, whatever it is assigned. The video record button is the spare: + RAW adds a RAW file to the next JPEG-only shot, Silent photography toggles the electronic shutter, or MY MENU opens your menu — and Access top item in MY MENU jumps straight to whatever you put first.

Step 4

The i menu is your dashboard

MENU→Custom Settings Menu→f1→Customize i menu

f1 Customize i menu builds the photo dashboard, g1 builds it for video, and the two layouts are separate, so the video dashboard can carry Frame size and rate, Microphone sensitivity and Electronic VR while the photo dashboard carries Focus mode, AF-area mode and Vibration reduction. Put the three you change most on the first row.

Try it now

Assign the Fn button only, first — Subject tracking — and shoot with it for a day. Add the AE-L/AF-L back-button focus pair when the Fn button is muscle memory, then the OK button and the lens control ring. Finish by building the i menu: Focus mode, AF-area mode and Vibration reduction on the first row.
