Autofocus, set up right

How do you set up autofocus and subject detection on a Nikon Z6 III so it holds your subject?

camerasdecoded.com/camera-select/nikon/z6-iii/autofocus
The answer — share this
Run AF-C with Wide-area AF (L) and Subject detection set to People — or Auto when the subject keeps changing — and let 3D-tracking carry anything that moves across the frame.
Focus mode: AF-C  ·  AF-area mode: Wide-area AF (L)  ·  Subject detection: People or Auto  ·  Set at: Photo Shooting Menu → AF/MF subject detection options
Cameras Decoded — Camera Select
Step 1

AF-C for movers, AF-S for still life

MENU→Photo Shooting Menu→Focus mode

AF-S locks focus at the half-press, and by default the camera will not fire until focus is achieved (focus priority). AF-C tracks continuously and, by default, fires the moment you press (release priority). There is no AF-A on the Z6 III — Nikon makes you choose, and anything that moves gets AF-C. AF-F exists too, but only in video mode.

Step 2

Name the subject

MENU→Photo Shooting Menu→AF/MF subject detection options

Subject detection offers Auto, People, Animal, Birds, Vehicle, Airplanes, and Subject detection off. Vehicle is a single category on this body — cars, motorcycles, trains, airplanes and bicycles are all detected under it. Animal covers dogs, cats and birds, faces first and then eyes; Birds is also a dedicated category with its own behaviour for feathered subjects. Auto lets the camera choose among humans, animals and vehicles. Remember: the Video Recording Menu keeps a separate detection choice of its own.

Know this: detection finds the subject's face first; when it can see the eyes, the focus point moves to an eye. Keep the category honest — People tuning on a dog gives you neither.
Step 3

Pick the area it hunts in

MENU→Photo Shooting Menu→AF-area mode

Wide-area AF (S) and (L) are the everyday areas, and Wide-area (C1)/(C2) let you build a custom-sized zone. 3D-tracking follows a subject you place the point on, across the whole frame. Dynamic-area AF (S/M/L) backs up a chosen point with its neighbours for predictable movers, and Pinpoint AF is the still-life specialist — a point smaller than Single-point, AF-S only. The boundary that matters: subject detection works only in Wide-area AF, 3D-tracking and Auto-area AF — never in Pinpoint or Single-point.

Step 4

Tune blocked shot response

MENU→Custom Settings Menu→a3 Focus tracking with lock-on

Blocked shot AF response sets how the camera reacts when something passes between you and your subject in AF-C. Toward 5 (Delayed), it holds on to your original subject through the interruption; toward 1 (Quick), it happily shifts to the crossing object. Note the fine print: with 3D-tracking selected, the behaviour is fixed at 3 regardless of this setting.

Try it now

Set AF-C, People, Wide-area AF (L) — then 3D-track a person crossing the room and feel the point travel. That is the setup working.
