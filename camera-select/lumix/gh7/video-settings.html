Video, set up right

How do you set up video on a Panasonic Lumix GH7 — 5.8K open gate, ProRes RAW in the camera, V-Log and ARRI LogC3, 32-bit float audio, and what the fan and heat settings actually do?

camerasdecoded.com/camera-select/lumix/gh7/video-settings
The answer — share this
Record MOV at 4:2:2 10-bit — up to 5.8K open gate, 5760×4320 in 4:3 at 200 Mbps from the full sensor — or Apple ProRes for in-camera RAW; know the 800 Mbps qualities need a CFexpress card, V-Log starts at ISO 500 with a LUT preview one menu away (or shoot REAL TIME LUT and bake the look), and long takes are a heat plan: Fan Mode, Thermal Management, and a battery that gives about 90 minutes of 4K.
Format: MOV  ·  Open gate: 5.8K/30p/420/10-L (4:3)  ·  800 Mbps = CFexpress only  ·  V-Log floor: ISO 500  ·  Assist: Custom (Monitor / Display (Video)) → Log View Assist
Cameras Decoded — Camera Select
Step 1

MOV, and the open gate

MENU→Video Menu (Image Format)→Rec Quality

Rec. File Format MOV carries the serious codecs: 4:2:2 10-bit ALL-Intra and Long GOP, with LPCM 4-channel audio. The flagship quality is 5.8K/30p/420/10-L — 5760×4320 pixels in 4:3 at 200 Mbps HEVC, recorded from the FULL image area: the open gate, the entire sensor height, ready to reframe for any delivery shape. 5.7K (5728×3024, 17:9) runs to 60p at 300 Mbps; C4K runs to 120p. Rec Quality (My List) registers up to 12 favorite combinations. Apple ProRes is the third format: ProRes 422 HQ and ProRes RAW HQ / ProRes RAW record in the camera — 5.7K/30p RAW HQ at 4.2 Gbps — and RAW video cannot be recorded to SD cards.

Know this: MOV at 800 Mbps (C4K/60p 422/10-I (H), FHD/240p), Apple ProRes above FHD, and ALL-Intra variable frame rate cannot be recorded to SD cards. Use a CFexpress card — the camera refuses the take otherwise.
Step 2

FULL or PIXEL/PIXEL — no crop surprise

MENU→Video Menu (Image Format)→Image Area of Video

Image Area of Video offers FULL (the whole Micro Four Thirds image circle) or PIXEL/PIXEL — a 1:1 pixel readout that narrows the angle of view, giving a telescopic effect without deterioration. Which areas are available depends on the Rec Quality. There is no APS-C area on this body: 4K 60p is recorded from the full sensor, so the frame you see is the frame you get at every frame rate.

Step 3

V-Log with a preview — or baked

MENU→Custom Menu (Monitor / Display (Video))→Log View Assist

With Photo Style set to V-Log, the available ISO range becomes 500–12800 at sensor output of 60 fps and below (250–3200 above 60 fps; the floor drops to 125 with Extended ISO ON), and 18% gray is exposed at IRE 42%. Log View Assist applies a LUT — Vlog_709 or any file registered in the LUT Library (up to 10 files, .vlt or .cube formats, loaded from a card or via LUMIX Lab) — to the monitor/viewfinder and HDMI output without touching the file; both assist switches ship OFF. REAL TIME LUT is the other road: a Photo Style that applies the LUT to the V-Log image as it records, for photos and video alike. ARRI LogC3 is the third log: EI 800, ISO floor 320, unlocked by the paid DMW-SFU3A upgrade key and activation. View Assist is not available while a LUT is baked into the Photo Style.

Step 4

Heat, planned

MENU→Setup Menu (Monitor / Display)→Thermal Management

There is no per-movie time cap; MOV files segment at about 3 h 04 min per file (192 GB at 600 Mbps or less, 640 GB at 800 Mbps and above). Heat is the real limit: when the camera temperature rises, a warning icon blinks, then recording and HDMI output stop until the body cools. At HIGH, recording continues longer while the body heats — Panasonic warns about low-temperature burns and recommends a tripod; handheld long recording belongs on STANDARD. Fan Mode (Custom Menu, IN/OUT) ships on AUTO2; AUTO1 prioritizes controlling the temperature rise. Panasonic rates the DMW-BLK22 battery at about 90 minutes of continuous 4K/60p recording, 50 minutes at 5.7K/60p and 40 minutes at C4K/120p (actual available time roughly half).

Step 5

Audio that forgives

MENU→Video Menu (Audio)→Sound Rec Quality

With the DMW-XLR2 XLR Microphone Adaptor attached, Sound Rec Quality offers 96 kHz/32-bit float (4-channel recording OFF) or 48 kHz/32-bit float (4-channel XLR or OFF) — float audio preserves peaks that would clip a normal recording. 4ch Audio Recording sends XLR inputs to CH1–CH4, or XLR to CH1/CH2 with the camera or external mic on CH3/CH4. The dedicated Audio information button shows all audio settings on one touch-changeable screen. MP4 fixes audio at 48 kHz/16-bit and records no time code; MOV and Apple ProRes record time code automatically. The older DMW-XLR1 adaptor cannot do 32-bit float or 4-channel XLR.

Try it now

Set MOV and the 5.8K open gate, turn Log View Assist on with Vlog_709, then flip Image Area of Video between FULL and PIXEL/PIXEL and watch the frame change. Finish by reading the Thermal Management screen for your long-take setting.
